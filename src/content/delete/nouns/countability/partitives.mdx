Partitives are words and expressions used to talk about a specific amount or part of something, especially with uncountable nouns.

Because uncountable nouns cannot normally be counted directly, we can use a partitive expression to make them countable.

- #### Compare:
  - some water
  - a glass of water
  - some bread
  - a slice of bread
  - some advice
  - a piece of advice

---

## A Piece of

A piece of is one of the most common partitives. It can be used with many uncountable nouns.

- a piece of advice.
- a piece of information.
- a piece of news.
- a piece of cake.
- a piece of paper.

### For example:

- She gave me a piece of advice.
- I need a piece of information.
- Would you like a piece of cake?

The plural form is pieces of.

- I need two pieces of information.

---

## Containers

We can use containers as partitives when we want to specify the amount of an uncountable noun.

Common expressions include:

- a glass of water.
- a bottle of milk.
- a cup of coffee.
- a bowl of rice.
- a box of cereal.
- a bag of flour.

### Examples:

- I drank a glass of water.
- She bought a bottle of milk.
- We had a bowl of rice for lunch.

---

## Portions and Pieces

Some partitives describe a portion or a piece of something.

### A Slice of

Use a slice of for food that can be cut into thin pieces.

- a slice of bread.
- a slice of cheese.
- a slice of cake.
- a slice of pizza.

I'd like a slice of pizza.

---

### A Loaf of

Use a loaf of with bread.

- We bought a loaf of bread.

The plural form is:

- two loaves of bread

---

### A Piece of

Use a piece of for an individual piece of something.

- a piece of chocolate.
- a piece of cake.
- a piece of paper.

Can I have a piece of chocolate?

---

### Drinks

We can use containers to specify amounts of drinks.

- a cup of tea.
- a cup of coffee.
- a glass of water.
- a bottle of juice.
- a can of soda.

#### Examples:

- I'd like a cup of tea.
- He drank a bottle of water.
- She ordered a glass of juice.

---

## Partitives with Countable Nouns

Partitives are not limited to uncountable nouns. They can also describe groups or portions of countable nouns.

- a group of students
- a pair of shoes
- a bunch of flowers
- a pack of cards
- a set of keys

### Examples:

- I bought a pair of shoes.
- She gave me a bunch of flowers.
- There is a group of students outside.

---

## Singular and Plural

The partitive changes to the plural when we talk about more than one unit.

- #### Compare:
  - a piece of advice.
  - two pieces of advice.
  - a bottle of water.
  - three bottles of water.
  - a slice of bread.
  - four slices of bread.

Notice that the noun after of usually stays uncountable:

- ✅ two pieces of information.
- ❌ two pieces of informations.
- ✅ three bottles of water.
- ❌ three bottles of waters.

---

## Common Mistakes

Using an Uncountable Noun Directly

- ❌ I need two advices.
- ✅ I need two pieces of advice.

Adding -s to an Uncountable Noun

- ❌ She gave me three informations.
- ✅ She gave me three pieces of information.

Using the Wrong Partitive

- ❌ I bought a loaf of cheese.
- ✅ I bought a slice of cheese.
