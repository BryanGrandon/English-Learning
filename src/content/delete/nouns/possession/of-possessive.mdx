We can use of + noun to show possession, belonging, or a relationship between things.

The basic structure is:

- #### **_the + thing + of + possessor_**
  - the door of the house
  - This means that the door belongs to or is part of the house.

---

## Basic Structure

The of-possessive usually follows this pattern:

**_noun + of + noun_**

- #### Examples:
  - the door of the house
  - the name of the city
  - the color of the car
  - the end of the movie
  - the beginning of the lesson

---

## Possession with Things

The of-possessive is commonly used when the possessor is a thing, place, organization, or concept.

- the roof of the building
- the windows of the house
- the name of the company
- the history of the country
- the end of the story

The roof of the building is damaged.

---

## Possession with People

We can use of with people, but 's is usually more natural in many everyday situations.

- #### Compare:
  - John's car ✓
  - the car of John → less natural
- #### For people, 's is generally preferred.
  - Maria's brother is a doctor.
- #### rather than:
  - The brother of Maria is a doctor.

---

## Of-Possessive with Relationships

The of structure can be useful when describing relationships between things or parts.

- the capital of Chile
- the population of the city
- the name of the street
- the center of the town
- the history of the country

### Examples

- The color of the wall is beautiful.
- What is the name of the restaurant?
- We visited the center of the city.
- The end of the movie was surprising.
- I know the history of this place.

---

## 's or Of?

Both structures can sometimes express possession, but they are not always equally natural.

| Possessive 's      | Of-possessive             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Sarah's book       | the cover of the book     |
| John's car         | the door of the car       |
| the teacher's desk | the door of the classroom |
| Chile's capital    | the capital of Chile      |

In general, 's is common with people, while of is common with things and concepts.

---

## Quick Summary

Use of + noun to show a relationship between two nouns, especially when the possessor is a thing, place, organization, or concept.

- #### the + noun + of + noun
  - the name of the city
- #### For people, 's is often more natural:
  - Sofia's book
