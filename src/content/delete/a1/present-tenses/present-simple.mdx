The Present Simple is used to talk about things that happen regularly, facts, habits, and situations that are generally true.

## Affirmative Sentences

The basic structure is:

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Verb' fullWidth>

- #### I / You / We / They + base verb
  - I work in an office.
  - You play tennis.
  - We live in Chile.
  - They study English.
- #### He / She / It + verb + -s / -es
  - He works in an office.
  - She plays tennis.
  - It looks good.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Third Person Singular' fullWidth>

With he, she, and it, the verb usually takes -s.

- work → works
- splay → plays
- read → reads

Some verbs take -es:

- go → goes
- watch → watches
- finish → finishes

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

## Negative Sentences

We use do not (don't) and does not (doesn't) to make negative sentences.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Do/Does + Not + Verb' >

- #### I / You / We / They + don't + base verb
  - I don't like coffee.
  - You don't work here.
  - We don't watch TV.
  - They don't live in London.
- #### He / She / It + doesn't + base verb
  - He doesn't like coffee.
  - She doesn't work here.
  - It doesn't look good.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='important' title='Base Verb After Does' >

After doesn't, use the base form of the verb.

- ✅ She doesn't work here.
- ❌ She doesn't works here.

The -s is included in does, so it is not added to the main verb.

</LessonBlock>

## Questions

We use do and does to form Present Simple questions.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Do/Does + Subject + Verb?' >

- #### Do + I / you / we / they + base verb?
  - Do you like music?
  - Do they live here?
  - Do we need a ticket?
- #### Does + he / she / it + base verb?
  - Does he work here?
  - Does she speak English?
  - Does it look good?

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='important' title='Base Verb After Does' fullWidth>

After does, use the base form of the verb.

- ✅ Does she work here?
- ❌ Does she works here?

</LessonBlock>

## Short Answers

We can use do or does in short answers.

<LessonBlock type="comparison">

| Question               | Yes            | No               |
| ---------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| Do you work here?      | Yes, I do.     | No, I don't.     |
| Do they study English? | Yes, they do.  | No, they don't.  |
| Does he live here?     | Yes, he does.  | No, he doesn't.  |
| Does she play tennis?  | Yes, she does. | No, she doesn't. |

</LessonBlock>

## Wh- Questions

We can use question words with the Present Simple.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Wh- Word + Do/Does + Subject + Verb?' >

What / Where / When / Why / How + do/does + subject + base verb?

- Where do you live?
- What do they eat?
- When does he work?
- Why does she study English?
- How does it work?

</LessonBlock>

## Uses

We use the Present Simple for several common situations.

### 1. Habits and Routines

We use it for actions that happen regularly.

- I get up at 7:00.
- She goes to school every day.
- They play football on Saturdays.

### 2. Facts and General Truths

We use it for things that are generally or always true.

- Water boils at 100°C.
- The Earth moves around the Sun.
- Birds have wings.

### 3. Permanent or Long-Term Situations

We use it for situations that are generally stable or permanent.

- I live in Chile.
- She works at a bank.
- They own a house.

### 4. Preferences and Opinions

We commonly use the Present Simple with verbs such as like, love, hate, want, and prefer.

- I like pizza.
- He loves music.
- She wants a new phone.
- They prefer tea.

## Common Mistakes

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Adding -s with I, You, We, or They' fullWidth>

Do not add -s to the verb with I, you, we, or they.

- ✅ They work here.
- ❌ They works here.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title="Using -s After Does or Doesn't" fullWidth>

Do not add -s to the main verb after does or doesn't.

- ✅ Does he play football?
- ❌ Does he plays football?

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>
