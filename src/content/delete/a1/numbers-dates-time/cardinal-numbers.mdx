Cardinal numbers are numbers we use to count people, animals, objects, and things.

They answer the question "How many?"

- one book
- three students
- ten apples
- twenty cars

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Cardinal Numbers' fullWidth>

Cardinal numbers express quantity or amount.

How many? → Three books.

</LessonBlock>

## Teens Cardinal Numbers

The numbers 13 through 19 follow a simple pattern. They all end in **-teen**, which represents the numbers 13 through 19.

<LessonBlock type="rule" title="Forming Teen Numbers"   >

Base number + **_-teen_** = Teen number

- four + **_teen_** → _fourteen_
- six + **_teen_** → _sixteen_
- seven + **_teen_** → _seventeen_

</LessonBlock>

Learn the teen numbers from 13 to 19.

## Tens Cardinal Number

The numbers from 20 to 90 are the multiples of ten in English. Learning them will help you form larger numbers, such as twenty-one, thirty-five, and ninety-nine.

<LessonBlock type="rule" title="Forming Tens"  >

The multiples of ten usually end in _-ty_.

Base number + **_-ty_** = Tens number

- six + **_ty_** → _sixty_
- seven + **_ty_** → _seventy_

</LessonBlock>

## Compound Cardinal Numbers

The numbers from 21 to 99 are formed by combining a multiple of ten with a single-digit number.

<LessonBlock type="rule" title="Forming Compound Numbers"  >

Compound numbers from 21 to 99 are formed by combining the tens and the ones with a hyphen (-).

Tens + **_-_** + One = Compound number

- #### _Examples_
  - twenty + **_-_** + one → _twenty-one_
  - thirty + **_-_** + five → _thirty-five_
  - sixty + **_-_** + eight → _sixty-eight_

</LessonBlock>

Learn how to form compound numbers using a hyphen.

## Hundreds, Thousands, Millions

Learn how to express larger numbers in English by combining hundreds, thousands, and millions.

<LessonBlock type="rule" title="Combining Large Numbers"  >

Cardinal number + hundred / thousand / million

- one + **_hundred_** → _one hundred_
- two + **_thousand_** → _two thousand_
- ten + **_million_** → _ten million_

</LessonBlock>

---

<LessonBlock type='remember'>

**Hundred**, **thousand**, and **million** do not take **_-s_** when they follow a number.

</LessonBlock>

### Using "and" in Numbers

British and American English use 'and' differently when reading large numbers.

<LessonBlock type="rule" title="Using and in Large Numbers">

- #### British English
  - In British English, **and** is used _before tens or units_.
    - one hundred **and** twenty (120)
    - two thousand **and** five (2,005)

- #### America English
  - American English often omits it.
    - one hundred twenty (120)
    - two thousand five (2,005)

</LessonBlock>
