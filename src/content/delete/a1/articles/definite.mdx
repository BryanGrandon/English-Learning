The is the definite article. We use the when we talk about a specific person, animal, place, thing, or group that the listener or reader can identify.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Basic Rule' fullWidth>

Use the when both the speaker and the listener know which person or thing we are talking about.

the + noun

- Close the door. → We know which door.
- Where is the book? → We know which book.
- The teacher is in the classroom. → We know which teacher.

</LessonBlock>

---

## The With Singular and Plural Nouns

The can be used with both singular and plural nouns, as well as with uncountable nouns, when they are specific.

### Singular Nouns

Use **_the_** with a singular noun when you are talking about a **specific person, place, animal, object, or thing**.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='the + singular noun' fullWidth>

- the car outside
- the teacher in my class
- the book on the table

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>
---

### Plural Nouns

Use **_the_** with a plural noun when you are talking about **specific people, animals, objects, or things**.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='the + plural noun' fullWidth>

- the students in my class
- the books on the table
- the dogs in the park

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

### Uncountable Nouns

Use **_the_** with an uncountable noun when you are talking about a **specific amount or instance** of something that cannot usually be counted individually.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='the + uncountable noun'  fullWidth>

- the water in this bottle
- the information on this page
- the money I gave you

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## When to Use The

The definite article **the** is used when we are referring to a specific or identifiable person, thing, or place. Usually, the speaker and the listener know exactly what is being referred to.

### Specific Things

Use the when the noun refers to a specific person, animal, place, or thing.

<LessonBlock type="example">

- Please open the window.
- I like the blue shirt.
- Where is the bathroom?
- Can you pass me the salt?

</LessonBlock>

---

### Second Mention

Use the when we mention the same noun again.

<LessonBlock type="example">

- I saw a dog in the park.
- The dog was very friendly.

</LessonBlock>

The first mention uses a because the dog is not yet specific. The second mention uses the because we now know which dog.

---

### Unique Things

Use the for things that are considered unique or one of a kind in a particular context.

<LessonBlock type="example">

- the sun
- the Earth
- the internet

</LessonBlock>

---

## The With Superlatives

Use the before superlative adjectives.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='rule' title='the + superlative adjective' fullWidth >

- the biggest city
- the tallest building
- the best student
- the most interesting book

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='example' fullWidth>
- This is the best restaurant in town.
- She is the tallest student in the class.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## The With Ordinal Numbers

Use the before ordinal numbers.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='rule' title='the + ordinal number + noun' fullWidth>

- the first day
- the second chapter
- the third floor
- the last question

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='example' fullWidth>

- Today is the first day of school.
- Read the second paragraph.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

## The With Places and Geographical Names

Some geographical names commonly use the.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Geographical Names' >

Use the with:

- rivers → the Amazon
- seas → the Mediterranean Sea
- oceans → the Pacific Ocean
- mountain ranges → the Andes
- deserts → the Sahara
- groups of islands → the Philippines
- groups of countries → the United States

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type="example">

- The Amazon is a very large river.
- The Andes are in South America.
- The Pacific Ocean is enormous.

</LessonBlock>

## When Not to Use The

We do not use the with every noun.

### General Ideas

When talking about people or things in general, we usually do not use the.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' fullWidth >

- ❌ The dogs are friendly.
- ✅ Dogs are friendly.
- ❌ The books are useful.
- ✅ Books are useful.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type="comparison" fullWidth>

- Dogs are friendly. → dogs in general
- The dogs are friendly. → specific dogs

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

### Proper Names

Many proper nouns do not normally need the.

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Proper Names' >

- ❌ the Chile
- ✅ Chile
- ❌ the Maria
- ✅ Maria

</LessonBlock>

However, some proper names do use the, such as the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands.

---

## The vs. A / An

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Definite vs. Indefinite Articles' >

| Article | Meaning      | Example               |
| ------- | ------------ | --------------------- |
| a / an  | non-specific | I saw a dog.          |
| the     | specific     | The dog was friendly. |

- A / an → one, but not a specific one
- The → a specific one that we can identify

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='example' title='Compare' fullWidth>

- I need a pen. → Any pen is okay.
- I need the pen. → A specific pen.
- She bought a book. → We don't know which book.
- The book is very interesting. → We know which book.

</LessonBlock>

## Pronunciation of The

The pronunciation changes depending on the sound that follows it.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='/ðə/ → before a consonant sound' fullWidth>

- the book
- the car

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='/ðiː/ → before a vowel sound' fullWidth>

- the apple
- the orange

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='remember' fullWidth>

Use the when the noun is specific or identifiable.

- `the + specific noun`
- I closed the door.

Use a/an when the noun is not specific.

- I opened a door.

</LessonBlock>
