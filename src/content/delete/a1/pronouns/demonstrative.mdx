Demonstrative pronouns are words we use to point to or identify people, animals, things, or ideas. They show whether something is near or far from the speaker and whether it is singular or plural.

The four demonstrative pronouns are this, that, these, and those.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='The Four Demonstrative Pronouns' >

| Distance | Singular | Plural |
| -------- | -------- | ------ |
| Near     | this     | these  |
| Far      | that     | those  |

</LessonBlock>

## Demonstratives as Pronouns and Determiners

This, that, these, and those can be used in two ways. They can replace a noun or come before a noun.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Pronoun vs. Determiner' >

Before a noun → Demonstrative determiner

- this book
- that car
- these shoes
- those students

Without a noun → Demonstrative pronoun

- This is interesting.
- That is my car.
- These are mine.
- Those are expensive.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Near and Far

The choice between this/these and that/those depends on the distance from the speaker. This distance can be physical or conceptual.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='example' title='Near' fullWidth>

- This phone is mine.
- These shoes are new.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='example' title='Far' fullWidth>

- That building is old.
- Those cars are expensive.

</LessonBlock> 
</ContentGrid>
