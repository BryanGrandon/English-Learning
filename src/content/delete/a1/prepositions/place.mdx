Prepositions of place describe the location or position of a person, object, or place.

They usually come after the main verb, especially the verb **to be**.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + verb + preposition of place + noun' fullWidth>

- The book is on the table.
- The cat is under the chair.
- Sarah is in the classroom.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Common Prepositions of Place

The most common prepositions of place are in, on, at, under, above, below, next to, between, behind, and in front of.

### In

Use in when someone or something is inside an enclosed space or area.

- The keys are in the bag.
- She is in the kitchen.
- There is some water in the bottle.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='In' fullWidth>

in + enclosed space or area

</LessonBlock>

---

### On

Use on when someone or something is touching a surface.

- The phone is on the table.
- There is a picture on the wall.
- The book is on the desk.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='On' fullWidth>

on + surface

</LessonBlock>

---

### At

Use at to talk about a specific point or location.

- She is at the bus stop.
- He is at school.
- We are at the door.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='At' fullWidth>

at + specific point or location

</LessonBlock>

---

## Position Relative to Other Things

### Under

Use under when something is below and usually close to another thing.

- The shoes are under the bed.
- The cat is under the table.

### Above

Use above when something is higher than another thing.

- The lamp is above the table.
- The picture is above the sofa.

### Below

Use below when something is lower than another thing.

- The temperature is below zero.
- The shelf is below the window.

### Behind

Use behind when something is at the back of another thing.

- The car is behind the house.
- The teacher is behind the desk.

### In front of

Use in front of when something is at the front of another thing.

- The car is in front of the house.
- She is standing in front of the door.

### Next to

Use next to when something is beside or very close to another thing.

- The bank is next to the supermarket.
- Tom is sitting next to Sarah.

### Between

Use between when something is in the space separating two people or things.

- The school is between the bank and the library.
- The cat is between the chairs.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Position Prepositions' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Meaning                     | Example                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| under       | lower than                  | The bag is under the chair.                |
| above       | higher than                 | The clock is above the door.               |
| below       | lower than                  | The picture is below the clock.            |
| behind      | at the back of              | The car is behind the house.               |
| in front of | at the front of             | The car is in front of the house.          |
| next to     | beside                      | The lamp is next to the bed.               |
| between     | in the middle of two things | The shop is between the bank and the café. |

</LessonBlock>

## Prepositions of Pla[ce: Quick Comparison

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='In, On, and At' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Main idea                  | Example                       |
| ----------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| in          | inside an area or space    | The children are in the room. |
| on          | touching a surface         | The keys are on the table.    |
| at          | specific point or location | She is at the bus stop.       |

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Common Mistakes' fullWidth>

Do not confuse in, on, and at.

- ✅ The book is on the table.
- ❌ The book is in the table.
- ✅ She is in the classroom.
- ❌ She is on the classroom.
- ✅ He is at the bus stop.
- ❌ He is in the bus stop.

</LessonBlock>

## Questions with Where

We often use where to ask about location.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Asking About Location' fullWidth>

Where + be + subject?

- Where is the book?
- Where are the keys?
- Where is your brother?

</LessonBlock>

- Where is the cat? — It is under the table.
- Where are the keys? — They are in my bag.
- Where is the school? — It is next to the library.

<LessonBlock type='tip' title='Tip' fullWidth>

When answering a question with Where...?, use a preposition of place to explain the location.

- Where is the phone?
  - → It is on the desk.

</LessonBlock>

## Common Prepositions

### Nearby & Relative Position

| Preposition      | Meaning                      | Example                                           |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| next to / beside | At the side of.              | The supermarket is **next to** the bank.          |
| by               | Close to.                    | I always sit **by** the window.                   |
| between          | In the middle of two things. | The shop is **between** the bank and the station. |

---

### Front & Back Position

| Preposition | Meaning           | Example                                   |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| behind      | At the back of.   | The church is **behind** the school.      |
| in front of | At the front of.  | The hotel is **in front of** the station. |
| opposite    | Facing something. | The bank is **opposite** the market.      |

---

### Vertical Position

| Preposition | Meaning                         | Example                                     |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| under       | Lower than; directly below.     | The ball is **under** the chair.            |
| above       | Higher than; not touching.      | The clock is **above** the table.           |
| below       | Lower than; not directly under. | The basement is **below** the ground floor. |

---

## Key Differences

<ContentGrid>

<SectionCard title="Next to / Beside vs By">

- **Next to / Beside** → directly at the side of something.
- **By** → close to something, but not necessarily at its side.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="In Front of vs Opposite">

- **In front of** → ahead of something, but not necessarily facing it.
- **Opposite** → directly facing something across from it.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="Under vs Below">

- **Under** → directly beneath something.
- **Below** → at a lower level, not necessarily directly beneath.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="Above vs On">

- **On** → touching a surface.
- **Above** → higher than something, without touching it.

</SectionCard>

</ContentGrid>
