# English A1–A2 Grammar Notes

> Original study notes organized around the main grammar areas covered in the referenced A1–A2 material. This is a newly written guide, not a reproduction of the source.

## 1. Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns replace nouns and identify people, animals, or things.

| Subject | Object | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | yo / me |
| you | you | tú / usted / ustedes |
| he | him | él / lo |
| she | her | ella / la |
| it | it | ello / lo / la |
| we | us | nosotros / nos |
| they | them | ellos / los |

**Examples**

- I am a student.
- She likes him.
- They help us.

## 2. Verb To Be

The verb **be** is used to talk about identity, descriptions, states, locations, and more.

| Subject | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | am not |
| you | are | are not |
| he/she/it | is | is not |
| we/you/they | are | are not |

**Examples**

- I am a student.
- She is happy.
- They are at home.
- He isn't tired.

### Questions

**Am / Is / Are + subject...?**

- Are you ready?
- Is she your sister?
- Are they at school?

## 3. Articles

### Indefinite Articles: A / An

Use **a** before a consonant sound and **an** before a vowel sound.

- a book
- a university
- an apple
- an hour

### Definite Article: The

Use **the** when the listener or reader can identify the specific person or thing.

- The door is open.
- I saw a dog. The dog was friendly.

## 4. Demonstratives

| Near | Far |
|---|---|
| this — singular | that — singular |
| these — plural | those — plural |

- This is my book.
- These are my shoes.
- That is your car.
- Those are my friends.

## 5. There Is / There Are

Use **there is** with singular nouns and uncountable nouns.

Use **there are** with plural countable nouns.

- There is a book on the table.
- There is some water in the glass.
- There are three students in the room.

### Past

- There was a problem.
- There were two problems.

## 6. Have and Have Got

**Have** expresses possession, relationships, characteristics, and other meanings.

- I have a car.
- She has two brothers.

**Have got** is also common for possession.

- I have got a car.
- She has got two brothers.

## 7. Do as an Auxiliary

Use **do / does** to form questions and negatives in the present simple.

| Subject | Auxiliary |
|---|---|
| I/you/we/they | do |
| he/she/it | does |

- Do you like music?
- Does he work here?
- I don't like coffee.
- She doesn't play tennis.

After **does**, the main verb returns to its base form.

## 8. Present Simple

Use the present simple for:

- routines
- habits
- facts
- repeated actions
- schedules

### Affirmative

**Subject + base verb**

For **he, she, it**, normally add **-s** or **-es**.

- I work every day.
- She works every day.
- He watches TV.

### Negative

**Subject + do/does + not + base verb**

- I don't work on Sunday.
- She doesn't work on Sunday.

### Questions

**Do/Does + subject + base verb...?**

- Do you speak English?
- Does he live here?

## 9. Present Continuous

Use the present continuous for actions happening now and for some temporary or arranged future situations.

**Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing**

- I am studying.
- She is working.
- They are playing.

### Negative

- I am not sleeping.
- He isn't working.
- We aren't watching TV.

### Questions

- Are you studying?
- Is she cooking?
- What are they doing?

## 10. Past Simple

Use the past simple for completed actions in the past.

### Be

| Subject | Past |
|---|---|
| I/he/she/it | was |
| you/we/they | were |

- I was tired.
- They were at home.

### Other verbs

Regular verbs normally use **-ed**.

- work → worked
- play → played
- watch → watched

Some verbs are irregular.

- go → went
- have → had
- do → did
- see → saw

### Negative

**Subject + did not + base verb**

- I didn't work.
- She didn't go.

### Questions

**Did + subject + base verb...?**

- Did you study?
- Did he call you?

## 11. Past Continuous

Use the past continuous for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past.

**Subject + was/were + verb-ing**

- I was studying at eight.
- They were watching TV.

It can describe an action interrupted by another past action.

- I was sleeping when the phone rang.

## 12. Used To

Use **used to + base verb** for past habits or states that are no longer true.

- I used to play outside every afternoon.
- She used to live in Madrid.

## 13. Possessive Adjectives

| Pronoun | Possessive |
|---|---|
| I | my |
| you | your |
| he | his |
| she | her |
| it | its |
| we | our |
| they | their |

- This is my book.
- That is her car.
- Their house is large.

## 14. Possessive Nouns

Use **'s** to show possession.

- John's book
- Maria's phone
- the dog's tail

For regular plural nouns ending in **-s**, normally add only an apostrophe.

- my parents' house
- the students' books

## 15. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

### Countable

Countable nouns can be counted.

- one apple
- two apples
- three books

### Uncountable

Uncountable nouns are treated as substances, materials, concepts, or quantities that are not normally counted individually.

- water
- money
- information
- advice

Common quantity expressions include:

- some water
- a lot of money
- a piece of advice
- a bottle of water

## 16. Quantifiers

| Expression | Typical use |
|---|---|
| many | countable plural nouns |
| much | uncountable nouns |
| a few | countable plural nouns |
| a little | uncountable nouns |
| some | countable and uncountable |
| a lot of | countable and uncountable |

- many books
- much time
- a few friends
- a little money
- some apples
- a lot of work

## 17. Plural Nouns

Most nouns form the plural with **-s**.

- book → books
- car → cars

Some use **-es**.

- box → boxes
- watch → watches

Some nouns ending in consonant + **y** change to **-ies**.

- city → cities
- party → parties

Common irregular plurals include:

- man → men
- woman → women
- child → children
- tooth → teeth
- foot → feet
- mouse → mice

## 18. Prepositions of Time

### At

Use **at** for precise times and some fixed expressions.

- at 7:00
- at noon
- at night

### On

Use **on** for days and dates.

- on Monday
- on my birthday
- on July 10

### In

Use **in** for months, years, seasons, and longer periods.

- in January
- in 2026
- in summer
- in the morning

## 19. Prepositions of Place

Common prepositions include:

- in
- on
- at
- under
- above
- behind
- in front of
- next to
- between
- near

Examples:

- The keys are on the table.
- The cat is under the chair.
- The school is next to the library.

## 20. Prepositions of Movement

Common movement prepositions include:

- to
- from
- into
- out of
- through
- across
- along
- past

Examples:

- We walked to school.
- She came from Chile.
- They ran across the street.
- He walked through the park.

## 21. Question Words

| Word | Main meaning |
|---|---|
| who | person |
| what | thing / information |
| which | choice |
| where | place |
| when | time |
| why | reason |
| how | manner / method |

Examples:

- Who is she?
- What do you want?
- Which one do you prefer?
- Where do you live?
- When does the class start?
- Why are you late?
- How do you make it?

## 22. Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns.

- a big house
- a new car
- an interesting book

After **be**, an adjective can describe the subject.

- The house is big.
- She is intelligent.

### Adjective Order

When several adjectives appear before a noun, English normally follows a conventional order.

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Noun**

Example:

- a beautiful small old wooden house

## 23. Modal Verbs

Common beginner modal verbs include:

- **can** — ability / permission
- **must** — strong obligation
- **may** — permission / possibility

A modal is followed by the base form of the verb.

- I can swim.
- You must study.
- May I come in?

Do not use **do/does** to form questions with modal verbs.

- Can you help me?
- Must we leave?

## 24. Imperatives

Use the base form of a verb to give instructions, commands, or directions.

- Open the door.
- Sit down.
- Listen carefully.

Negative imperative:

**Don't + base verb**

- Don't touch that.
- Don't open the window.

## 25. Infinitives and -ing Forms

The infinitive commonly uses **to + base verb**.

- to work
- to study
- to learn

After some verbs, English uses an **-ing** form.

- I like reading.
- She enjoys cooking.

## 26. Numbers

### Cardinal Numbers

- 1 — one
- 2 — two
- 10 — ten
- 20 — twenty
- 30 — thirty
- 40 — forty
- 50 — fifty
- 100 — one hundred
- 1,000 — one thousand

Compound numbers from 21 to 99 normally use a hyphen in written English.

- twenty-one
- thirty-five
- ninety-nine

## 27. Telling the Time

Common expressions:

- It's three o'clock.
- It's half past four.
- It's a quarter past five.
- It's a quarter to six.

Use **at** with a specific time:

- The class starts at 8:30.

Examples:

## 29. Link Words

Common linking words for A1–A2 include:

- **and** — addition
- **but** — contrast
- **or** — alternative
- **because** — reason
- **so** — result
- **then** — sequence

Examples:

- I like tea and coffee.
- I wanted to go, but I was tired.
- We stayed home because it was raining.
- I was tired, so I went to bed.

## 30. Short Agreement: So / Neither

Use **so** to agree with a positive statement.

- I like pizza. — So do I.
- She is tired. — So am I.

Use **neither** to agree with a negative statement.

- I don't like coffee. — Neither do I.
- He isn't ready. — Neither am I.

The auxiliary changes according to the original sentence.
