## What Are Prepositions of Movement?

Prepositions of movement describe **movement from one place to another**.

Common prepositions of movement include:

> **to · from · into · out of · through · across**

Examples:

- She is walking **to** school.
- He is coming **from** work.
- The cat jumped **into** the box.

---

## To

Use **to** to show movement toward a place or destination.

### Structure

> **move + to + destination**

Examples:

- I go **to school** every day.
- She walks **to the park**.
- They are going **to the supermarket**.
- He is traveling **to London**.

Think:

> **to = toward a destination**

---

## From

Use **from** to show the starting point of movement.

Examples:

- I walk **from home** to school.
- She is coming **from the office**.
- They are traveling **from Chile** to Argentina.
- The train goes **from London** to Manchester.

A common pattern is:

> **from + starting point + to + destination**

Example:

> We travel **from Santiago to Rancagua**.

---

## Into

Use **into** when someone or something moves from outside to inside.

Examples:

- She walks **into the room**.
- The cat jumped **into the box**.
- He went **into the house**.

Compare:

> The cat is **in** the box. → location
> The cat jumps **into** the box. → movement

---

## Out Of

Use **out of** when someone or something moves from inside to outside.

Examples:

- She walks **out of the room**.
- He comes **out of the house**.
- The cat jumped **out of the box**.
- Take the books **out of the bag**.

Compare:

> The cat is **in** the box. → location
> The cat jumps **out of** the box. → movement

---

## Through

Use **through** when someone or something moves from one side of an enclosed space to the other side.

Examples:

- We walk **through the park**.
- The train goes **through the tunnel**.
- She walks **through the door**.
- The river flows **through the city**.

Think:

> **through = from one side to another inside a space**

---

## Across

Use **across** when someone or something moves from one side of a surface or area to the other.

Examples:

- We walk **across the street**.
- She runs **across the park**.
- They walk **across the bridge**.
- He swims **across the river**.

Think:

> **across = from one side to the other**

---

## To vs. Into

These prepositions are related but have different meanings.

**To** shows movement toward a destination:

> She walks **to the house**.

**Into** shows movement from outside to inside:

> She walks **into the house**.

Compare:

- He goes **to the room**. → destination
- He goes **into the room**. → enters the room

---

## In vs. Into

**In** describes location.

> The children are **in the classroom**.

**Into** describes movement.

> The children walk **into the classroom**.

---

## Common Prepositions of Movement

| Preposition | Meaning                         | Example                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **to**      | toward a destination            | Go to school.            |
| **from**    | starting point                  | Come from work.          |
| **into**    | outside → inside                | Walk into the room.      |
| **out of**  | inside → outside                | Walk out of the room.    |
| **through** | from one side to another inside | Walk through the tunnel. |
| **across**  | from one side to another        | Walk across the street.  |

---

## Common Mistakes

### To vs. Into

- ❌ She walks into school every morning.
  - -> This can be correct if the meaning is that she enters the building.

For simple destination:

- ✅ She walks to school every morning.

### In vs. Into

- ❌ He walks in the room.
  - -> This normally means he walks while he is inside the room.
- ✅ He walks into the room.
  - -> He enters the room.

### From and To

Use **from** for the starting point and **to** for the destination.

> I travel **from** Chile **to** Argentina.

---

## Quick Summary

- **to** → destination
- **from** → starting point
- **into** → movement inside
- **out of** → movement outside
- **through** → movement through an enclosed space
- **across** → movement from one side to another

### Basic Pattern

> **from + starting point + to + destination**

**Example:** She travels from Chile to Argentina.

> **movement + into/out of + place**

**Example:** He walks into the room.
