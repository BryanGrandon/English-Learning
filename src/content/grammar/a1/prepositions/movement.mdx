Prepositions of movement and direction are words we use to describe where someone or something moves.

They show the direction or path of a movement.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Basic Structure' fullWidth>

Subject + verb of movement + preposition + destination or direction

- She is walking to school.
- He ran into the house.
- They walked through the park.

</LessonBlock>

---

## To and From

### To

Use to to show movement toward a destination or place.

- I walk to school every day.
- She is going to the store.
- We traveled to London.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='to + destination' fullWidth>

- go to school
- walk to the park
- travel to another city

</LessonBlock>

---

### From

Use from to show the starting point of a movement.

- I walk from school to the park.
- She is coming from the store.
- We traveled from Chile to Argentina.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='from + starting point' fullWidth>

- from home
- from school
- from the airport

</LessonBlock>

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='To vs. From' fullWidth>

Preposition Meaning Example
to destination We are going to school.
from starting point We are coming from school.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Into and Out of

### Into

Use into to show movement from outside to inside a place or thing.

- The cat jumped into the box.
- She walked into the room.
- He put the books into his bag.

---

### Out of

Use out of to show movement from inside to outside.

- The cat jumped out of the box.
- She walked out of the room.
- He took the books out of his bag.

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Into vs. Out of' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Meaning          | Example                    |
| ----------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| into        | outside → inside | He walked into the room.   |
| out of      | inside → outside | He walked out of the room. |

</LessonBlock>

---

## Onto and Off

### Onto

Use onto to show movement to a position on a surface.

- The cat jumped onto the table.
- She climbed onto the bed.
- He stepped onto the platform.

### Off

Use off to show movement away from a surface.

- The cat jumped off the table.
- She got off the bed.
- He stepped off the platform.

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Onto vs. Off' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Meaning                      | Example                        |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| onto        | movement to a surface        | The cat jumped onto the table. |
| off         | movement away from a surface | The cat jumped off the table.  |

</LessonBlock>

---

## Through and Across

### Through

Use through when someone or something moves from one side to the other inside a space or area.

- We walked through the park.
- The train went through the tunnel.
- She ran through the door.

---

### Across

Use across when someone or something moves from one side to the other over a surface or area.

- We walked across the street.
- She ran across the field.
- They swam across the river.

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Through vs. Across' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Main idea                               | Example                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| through     | inside a space from one side to another | We walked through the tunnel. |
| across      | over an area from one side to another   | We walked across the street.  |

</LessonBlock>

## Along and Around

### Along

Use along when moving following the length or direction of something.

- We walked along the river.
- They drove along the road.
- She ran along the beach.

---

### Around

Use around when moving in a circle or surrounding something.

- We walked around the park.
- The children ran around the house.
- The Earth moves around the Sun.

---

### Past

Use past when moving from one side of something to the other while going beyond it.

- Walk past the bank.
- We drove past the school.
- She walked past me.

<LessonBlock type='example' title='Past' fullWidth>

- Go past the supermarket and turn left.
- Here, past means to move beyond the supermarket.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Up and Down

### Up

Use up to describe movement toward a higher position.

- She walked up the stairs.
- They climbed up the hill.
- He ran up the street.

### Down

Use down to describe movement toward a lower position.

- She walked down the stairs.
- They ran down the hill.
- He walked down the street.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Up vs. Down' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Direction                | Example               |
| ----------- | ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| up          | toward a higher position | Walk up the stairs.   |
| down        | toward a lower position  | Walk down the stairs. |

</LessonBlock>

</LessonBlock>

## Questions with Where

We can use where to ask about the destination or direction of movement.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Asking About Movement' fullWidth>

Where + be + subject + going?

- Where are you going?
- Where is she going?
- Where are they going?

Where + do/does + subject + verb?

- Where do you walk?
- Where does he go?

</LessonBlock>

- Where are you going? — I'm going to school.
- Where is she going? — She's going into the kitchen.
- Where are they walking? — They're walking through the park.

## Common Mistakes

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='To vs. In' fullWidth>

Use to for movement toward a destination and in for location.

- ✅ I am going to the kitchen.
- ✅ I am in the kitchen.
- ❌ I am going in the kitchen.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Into vs. In' fullWidth>

Into describes movement. In describes location.

- ✅ She walked into the room.
- ✅ She is in the room.
- ❌ She walked in the room. (when you mean movement from outside to inside)

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='To vs. At' fullWidth>

To describes movement toward a destination. At describes a location.

- ✅ He is going to school.
- ✅ He is at school.
- ❌ He is going at school.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>
