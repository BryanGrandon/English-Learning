Prepositions of time are words we use to talk about when something happens.

The most common prepositions of time are in, on, and at.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + verb + preposition of time + time expression' fullWidth>

- I study in the morning.
- We have English class on Monday.
- She gets up at 7:00.

</LessonBlock>

---

## In, On, and At

### In

Use in with longer periods of time, such as months, years, seasons, centuries, and parts of the day.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type="usage" title='Use in with' fullWidth>

- Months: in January, in July
- Years: in 2026, in 1999
- Seasons: in summer, in winter
- Parts of the day: in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='example' fullWidth>

- My birthday is in July.
- She was born in 2010.
- We go to the beach in summer.
- I study in the evening.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

### On

Use on with days and dates.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type="usage" title='Use in with' fullWidth>

- Days: on Monday, on Friday
- Dates: on July 15th, on December 25th
- Specific days: on my birthday, on the weekend

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='example' fullWidth>

- I have a test on Monday.
- The party is on Saturday.
- Christmas is on December 25th.
- We visited our grandparents on my birthday.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

### At

Use at with specific times and points in time.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type="usage" title='Use in with' fullWidth>

- Clock times: at 7:00, at 10:30
- Specific points in time: at noon, at midnight
- Some fixed expressions: at night, at the weekend

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='example' fullWidth>

- The class starts at 8:00.
- I go to bed at 10:00.
- We have lunch at noon.
- She studies at night.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='In, On, and At' >

| Preposition | Use                                      | Example   |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------- |
| in          | months, years, seasons, parts of the day | in July   |
| on          | days and dates                           | on Monday |
| at          | specific times                           | at 7:00   |

Think of it as:

- in → longer period
- on → day/date
- at → exact time

</LessonBlock>

## Other Prepositions of Time

### Before

Use before to say that something happens earlier than another time or event.

- I eat breakfast before school.
- Wash your hands before dinner.
- She arrived before 8:00.

---

### After

Use after to say that something happens later than another time or event.

- I play games after school.
- We go home after class.
- She called me after dinner.

---

### During

Use during to say that something happens within a period of time or event.

- I read during the afternoon.
- Don't talk during the movie.
- We studied during the lesson.

---

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Before, After, and During' >

| Preposition | Meaning         | Example          |
| ----------- | --------------- | ---------------- |
| before      | earlier than    | before school    |
| after       | later than      | after school     |
| during      | within a period | during the class |

</LessonBlock>

---

## From ... To

Use from ... to to show the beginning and end of a period.

- I work from 9:00 to 5:00.
- The course runs from Monday to Friday.
- The store is open from 8:00 to 10:00.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='from + starting time + to + ending time' fullWidth>

- from 8:00 to 12:00
- from Monday to Friday
- from January to March

</LessonBlock>

### Until

Use until to say that something continues up to a particular time.

- I study until 9:00.
- The shop is open until midnight.
- We waited until Monday.

### For

Use for to talk about the duration of an action or situation.

- I studied for two hours.
- She stayed there for a week.
- We waited for ten minutes.

### Since

Use since to show the starting point of a period that continues to the present.

- I have lived here since 2020.
- She has been here since Monday.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='For and Since' fullWidth>

| Preposition | Use            | Example       |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| for         | duration       | for two hours |
| since       | starting point | since Monday  |

</LessonBlock>

## Questions with When

We often use when to ask about the time something happens.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Asking About Time' fullWidth>

When + do/does + subject + verb?

- When do you study?
- When does the class start?

With be: When + be + subject?

- When is the test?
- When is your birthday?

</LessonBlock>

- When do you go to school? — I go at 8:00.
- When is your birthday? — It's in July.
- When is the meeting? — It's on Monday.

## Common Mistakes

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='In, On, and At' fullWidth>

Be careful with in, on, and at.

- ✅ I was born in 2010.
- ❌ I was born on 2010.
- ✅ The test is on Monday.
- ❌ The test is in Monday.
- ✅ The class starts at 9:00.
- ❌ The class starts on 9:00.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Time Expressions Without a Preposition' fullWidth>

Some common time expressions usually do not use in, on, or at.

- ✅ today
- ✅ tomorrow
- ✅ yesterday
- ✅ this morning
- ✅ next week
- ✅ last year
- ❌ on tomorrow
- ❌ in next week

</LessonBlock>
