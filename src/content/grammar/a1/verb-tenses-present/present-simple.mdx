# Present Simple

The Present Simple is used to talk about:

- Daily routines
- Habits
- General facts

<Callout type='example'>

- I study English
- She works in a hospital

</Callout>

---

## Form (Grammar Rules)

<Grammar type='rule' title="Grammar Rule">

- **I / You / We / They → base verb**
- **He / She / It → verb + “-s”**
- #### Examples
  - I work
  - She works
  - They play
  - He plays

</Grammar>

---

## Sentence Structure

### Affirmative Sentences

<Grammar type='structure' title='Affirmative' highlight='subject + verb'>
- #### Examples
  - I eat vegetables
  - He plays football
  - They learn English

</Grammar>

---

### Negative Sentences

<Grammar type='structure' title='Negative' highlight='subject + do/does + not + verb'>

- #### Examples
  - I do not (don’t) eat vegetables
  - He does not (doesn’t) play football

</Grammar>

---

### Interrogative Sentences

<Grammar type='structure' title='Interrogative' highlight='do/does + subject + verb'>

- #### Examples
  - Do you eat vegetables?
  - Does he play football?

</Grammar>

---

### Important Rule

In negatives and questions:

- The **auxiliary (do/does)** changes
- The **main verb stays in base form**

---

## Uses of Present Simple

### Routines and habits

- I wake up at 7:00
- She always studies at night

Common adverbs:

- always, usually, often, sometimes, never

### General facts

- The sun rises in the east
- Elephants live in Africa

### Scheduled future events

- The train leaves at 10:00
- The party is tonight

---

## Adverbs of Frequency

Usually go before the verb

- I always eat breakfast
- She often studies

Exception with **to be**:

- I am always happy
- He is often tired

---

## Quick Summary

- He/She/It → add **s**
- Negative → **do/does + not**
- Questions → **do/does at the beginning**
- Used for habits, facts, schedules
