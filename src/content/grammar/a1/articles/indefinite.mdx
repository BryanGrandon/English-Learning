A and an are the indefinite articles. We use them before singular countable nouns when we talk about a person, animal, place, or thing in a general or non-specific way.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='a / an + singular countable noun' >

Use a or an before a singular countable noun when it is not specific.

- a book
- a teacher
- an apple
- an umbrella

</LessonBlock>

---

## A or An

The choice between a and an depends on the sound that comes immediately after the article.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='A + Consonant Sound' fullWidth>

Use a before a consonant sound.

- a book
- a car
- a dog
- a teacher
- a university

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='An + Vowel Sound' fullWidth>

Use an before a vowel sound.

- an apple
- an egg
- an orange
- an umbrella
- an hour

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

### A and An With Non-Specific Nouns

Use a or an when we do not identify a particular person or thing.

<LessonBlock type='usage' title='One of Many' fullWidth>

A and an can mean one person or thing among many.

- I need a pen. → Any pen is okay.
- She wants a dog. → Any dog.
- He ate an apple. → One apple, not a specific one.
- I saw a bird in the garden. → We don't know which bird.

</LessonBlock>

---

### First Mention

Use a or an when introducing a singular countable noun for the first time.

<LessonBlock type='example' title='First and Second Mention' fullWidth>

- I saw a dog in the park.
- The dog was very friendly.

We use a because the dog is not yet identified. Once we know which dog we are talking about, we use the.

</LessonBlock>

---

### A and An With Jobs and Professions

Use a or an before a singular job or profession.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Jobs and Professions' fullWidth>

be + a/an + profession

- She is a teacher.
- He is a doctor.
- My sister is an engineer.
- He wants to be an actor.

</LessonBlock>

---

### A and An With Adjectives

When an adjective comes before the noun, the article comes before the adjective.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='A/An + Adjective + Noun' fullWidth>

a/an + adjective + singular countable noun

- a big house
- a new car
- an interesting book
- an old computer

The choice depends on the sound of the adjective, not the noun.

- a beautiful house
- an expensive house

</LessonBlock>

---

## The Sound, Not the Letter

The choice between a and an is based on pronunciation, not spelling.

<LessonBlock type='important' title='Focus on the Sound' fullWidth>

Use a before a consonant sound:

- a university → /juː.../
- a European country → /jʊə.../

Use an before a vowel sound:

- an hour → /aʊ.../
- an honest person → /ɒn.../

Even though university starts with the letter u, it begins with a consonant sound.

Even though hour starts with the letter h, the h is silent and the word begins with a vowel sound.

</LessonBlock>

---

## When Not to Use A or An

A and an are only used with singular countable nouns.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Plural Nouns' fullWidth>

Do not use a/an with plural nouns.

- ❌ a books
- ✅ books
- ❌ an apples
- ✅ apples

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Uncountable Nouns' fullWidth>

Do not normally use a/an directly before an uncountable noun.

- ❌ a water
- ✅ water
- ❌ an information
- ✅ some information

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## A/An vs. The

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Indefinite vs. Definite Article'>

| Article | Use          | Example               |
| ------- | ------------ | --------------------- |
| a/an    | Non-specific | I saw a dog.          |
| the     | Specific     | The dog was friendly. |

- a/an → we don't know exactly which one
- the → we know which one

</LessonBlock>

### Common Examples

<LessonBlock type='example' title='Examples in Sentences' fullWidth>

- I have a car.
- She bought an umbrella.
- He is a student.
- My father is an engineer.
- We saw a bird in the garden.
- She wants an orange.
- They live in a small apartment.
- He found an interesting book.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='remember' fullWidth>

- #### A → before a consonant sound
  - a book · a university · a European country
- #### An → before a vowel sound
  - an apple · an hour · an interesting book

Both are used with singular countable nouns when the noun is not specific.

</LessonBlock>
