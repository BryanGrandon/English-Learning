<LessonHeader title='Singular and Plural Nouns' translation='Sustantivos en singular y plural' />

A noun can be singular or plural. A **singular noun** refers to **_one_** person, place, animal, thing, or idea,
while a **plural noun** refers to **_two or more_** people, places, animals, things, or ideas.

<Grammar type="comparison" title='Singular vs. Plural Nouns' >

| Singular | Plural   |
| -------- | -------- |
| book     | books    |
| student  | students |
| car      | cars     |
| apple    | apples   |

</Grammar>

<ContentGrid>

<SectionCard title='1. Adding -s'>

Most nouns form the plural by adding **-s**.

<Grammar type='rule' title='Regular Plural Nouns' highlight='Noun + s' fullWidth={true}>

- #### Examples
  - car → cars
  - house → houses
  - pen → pens
  - book → books

</Grammar>

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title='2. Adding -es'>

If a noun ends in **s, ss, sh, ch, x, or o**, add **-es**.

<Grammar type='rule' title='' highlight='Noun + es' fullWidth={true}>

- #### Examples
  - bus → buses
  - box → boxes
  - watch → watches
  - dish → dishes

</Grammar>

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title='3. Nouns Ending in -y'>

If a noun ends in **consonant + y**, change **y** to **ies**.

<Grammar type='rule' title='Consonant + y → ies' highlight='Consonant + y → ies' fullWidth={true}>

- #### Examples
  - party → parties
  - city → cities
  - baby → babies
  - country → countries

</Grammar>

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title='4. Nouns Ending in -f or -fe'>

Some nouns ending in **f** or **fe** change to **ves** in the plural.

<Grammar type='rule' title='Add -s' highlight='f / fe → ves' fullWidth={true}>

- #### Examples
  - leaf → leaves
  - wife → wives
  - life → lives
  - wolf → wolves

</Grammar>

</SectionCard>

</ContentGrid>
