Possessive nouns show that something belongs to or is connected to a person, animal, place, or thing.

We usually form possessive nouns by adding **_'s_** or **_'_** to a noun.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Form' >

Noun + 's + thing/person

- Sarah's book → the book belongs to Sarah.
- the dog's toy → the toy belongs to the dog.
- my brother's room → the room belongs to my brother.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Singular Nouns

For most singular nouns, **_add 's_** to show possession.

<ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='rule' title="Singular Noun + 's" fullWidth>

Add 's to a singular noun.

- the girl's bag
- John's car
- the teacher's desk
- my mother's phone

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='example' title='In Sentences' fullWidth>

- This is Anna's book.
- That is the dog's bed.
- Tom's bicycle is new.

</LessonBlock>

</ContentGrid>

---

## Plural Nouns Ending in -s

For regular plural nouns that already end in -s, add only an apostrophe (').

<LessonBlock type='rule' title="Plural Noun + '" >

Add an apostrophe after the final -s.

- the students' books
- the teachers' room
- the dogs' toys
- my parents' house

</LessonBlock>

---

## Possession with Two Nouns

When two or more people share the same thing, we normally add 's only to the last noun.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Shared Possession' fullWidth>

Person + and + Person's + thing

- Tom and Sarah's house → they share one house.
- John and Mary's car → they share one car.

If each person has a different thing, use the possessive form for each noun:

- Tom's and Sarah's houses → they have different houses.
- John's and Mary's cars → they have different cars.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Possessive Nouns vs. Plural Nouns

An apostrophe can change the meaning of a noun.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Plural vs. Possessive' fullWidth>

Form Meaning Example

- students more than one student The students are here.
- student's belonging to one student The student's book is here.
- students' belonging to multiple students The students' books are here.

</LessonBlock>

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title="Don't Confuse Plurals and Possessives" fullWidth>

- ❌ The students book is here.
- ✅ The student's book is here.
- ❌ The students's books are here.
- ✅ The students' books are here.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type="remember" fullWidth>

- Plural: students
- Singular possessive: student's
- Plural possessive: students'

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Possessive Nouns in Sentences

Possessive nouns show that something **belongs to or is connected to** someone or something.

<LessonBlock type='example' title='Examples' fullWidth>

- **Maria's** brother is a doctor.
- The **cat's** tail is long.
- My **parents'** house is in Chile.
- The **children's** toys are on the floor.
- The **teachers'** office is upstairs.
- **James's** phone is on the table.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='important' title='Remember' fullWidth>

- Singular noun → 's
- Regular plural noun ending in -s → '
- Irregular plural noun → 's

</LessonBlock>
