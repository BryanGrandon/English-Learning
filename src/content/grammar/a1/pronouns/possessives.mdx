Possessives are used to show that something belongs to or is connected to a person, animal, or thing.

In English, possession can be expressed in three main ways: possessive adjectives, possessive pronouns, and possessive nouns.

<ContentGrid> 
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Adjectives' fullWidth>

Possessive adjectives are used before a noun to show who something belongs to.

- **_my_** book
- **_your_** phone
- **_his_** car
- **_her_** bag
- **_its_** tail
- **_our_** house
- **_their_** dog

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Pronouns' fullWidth>

Possessive pronouns show possession and replace the noun.

- This book is **_mine_**.
- That phone is **_yours_**.
- The blue car is **_his_**.
- The red bag is **_hers_**.
- This house is **_ours_**.
- Those keys are **_theirs_**.

</LessonBlock> 
</ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Nouns' fullWidth>

Possessive nouns use an apostrophe (') to show that something belongs to someone or something.

- Maria's book
- the dog's toy
- the students' classroom
- the children's toys

</LessonBlock>

## Possessive Forms

The form we use depends on the structure of the sentence.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Three Ways to Show Possession' >

| Type                 | Structure                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Possessive adjective | possessive adjective + noun |
| Possessive pronoun   | possessive pronoun          |
| Possessive noun      | possessive noun + noun      |

</LessonBlock>

---

## Possessive Adjectives

Use a possessive adjective before a noun to show who or what it belongs to.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Adjective + Noun' >

- my sister
- your name
- his computer
- her room
- its color
- our school
- their teacher

</LessonBlock>

---

## Possessive Pronouns

Use a possessive pronoun when the noun is already understood and does not need to be repeated.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Possessive Pronoun' >

- This book is mine.
- That pencil is yours.
- The blue car is his.
- The red bag is hers.
- This house is ours.
- Those seats are theirs.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Possessive Nouns

Use a possessive noun to show that a person, animal, or thing owns or is connected to something.

<ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='structure' title="Possessive Noun + 's" fullWidth>

For most singular nouns, add 's.

- Maria → Maria's book
- teacher → teacher's desk
- dog → dog's toy

</LessonBlock> 
<LessonBlock type='structure' title="Plural Noun + '" fullWidth>

For regular plural nouns ending in -s, add only an apostrophe.

- students → students' books
- teachers → teachers' room
- dogs → dogs' toys

</LessonBlock> 
<LessonBlock type='structure' title="Irregular Plural + 's" fullWidth>

For irregular plural nouns that do not end in -s, add 's.

- children → children's toys
- men → men's clothes
- women → women's shoes

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Possessives: Quick Comparison

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Which Form Should I Use?' fullWidth >

| Form       | Use                         |
| ---------- | --------------------------- |
| my         | before a noun               |
| mine       | replaces a noun             |
| Maria's    | shows possession            |
| students'  | plural possessive           |
| children's | irregular plural possessive |

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Possessive Adjectives vs. Pronouns' fullWidth>

Do not confuse possessive adjectives with possessive pronouns.

- ❌ This is mine book.
- ✅ This is my book.
- ✅ This book is mine.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='remember' title='Remember' fullWidth >

There are three main ways to express possession:

- Possessive adjective + noun → my book
- Possessive pronoun → The book is mine.
- Possessive noun + noun → Maria's book

</LessonBlock>
