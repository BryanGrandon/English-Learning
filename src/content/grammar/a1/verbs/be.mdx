The verb be is one of the most important verbs in English. It is used to talk about people, things, places, states, and characteristics.

Unlike most English verbs, be changes form depending on the subject.

---

## Present forms of Be

In the present, be has three forms:

- am → with I
- is → with he, she, it
- are → with you, we, they

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Present Forms' >

| Subject | Be  | Example            |
| ------- | --- | ------------------ |
| I       | am  | I am a student.    |
| You     | are | You are my friend. |
| He      | is  | He is tired.       |
| She     | is  | She is happy.      |
| It      | is  | It is cold.        |
| We      | are | We are ready.      |
| They    | are | They are at home.  |

</LessonBlock>

---

## Affirmative sentences

Use am, is, or are to make affirmative sentences.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Be + Complement' fullWidth>

Subject + am/is/are + complement

- I am a student.
- She is happy.
- They are at school.
- We are friends.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Contractions

In everyday English, the forms of be are often contracted.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Common Contractions' >

| Full form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| I am      | I'm         |
| You are   | You're      |
| He is     | He's        |
| She is    | She's       |
| It is     | It's        |
| We are    | We're       |
| They are  | They're     |

</LessonBlock>

- I am happy. → I'm happy.
- She is a teacher. → She's a teacher.
- They are ready. → They're ready.

---

## Negative sentences

To make a negative sentence, add not after am, is, or are.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Be + Not' fullWidth>

Subject + am/is/are + not + complement

- I am not tired.
- He is not at home.
- We are not ready.
- They are not students.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type="note" fullWidth>

| Full form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| am not    | I'm not     |
| is not    | isn't       |
| are not   | aren't      |

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Avoid This Mistake' fullWidth>

Do not use do/does to make negative sentences with be.

- ❌ I don't be tired.
- ✅ I am not tired.
- ❌ She doesn't be happy.
- ✅ She isn't happy.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Questions

To make a question with be, put am, is, or are before the subject.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Be + Subject + Complement?' >

Am/Is/Are + subject + complement?

- Are you a student?
- Is she your sister?
- Are they ready?
- Is he at home?

</LessonBlock>

### Short answers

Short answers use be without repeating the complement.

<LessonBlock type="structure" title='Short Answers' >

- #### Are you tired?
  - → Yes, I am.
  - → No, I'm not.
- #### Is she a teacher?
  - → Yes, she is.
  - → No, she isn't.
- #### Are they ready?
  - → Yes, they are.
  - → No, they aren't.

</LessonBlock>

## Uses of Be

The verb be has several common uses.

### 1. Identity and occupation

Use be to say who someone is or what their occupation is.

- I am Bryan.
- She is a doctor.
- They are students.

---

### 2. Characteristics and states

Use be to describe characteristics, feelings, or states.

- He is tall.
- I am tired.
- We are happy.
- The food is hot.

---

### 3. Location

Use be to say where someone or something is.

- She is at home.
- The books are on the table.
- We are in Chile.

---

### 4. Age

English uses be to talk about age.

- I am thirteen years old.
- She is twenty years old.
- They are ten years old.

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Be for Age' fullWidth>

In English, we use be, not have, to express age.

- ❌ I have thirteen years.
- ✅ I am thirteen years old.

</LessonBlock>
