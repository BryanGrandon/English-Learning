he verb do has two main uses in English:

- As a main verb → means “hacer”.
- As an auxiliary verb → helps form questions and negative sentences in the present simple.

---

## Do as a Main Verb

When do means “hacer”, it is the main verb of the sentence.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Present Forms' fullWidth>

| Subject             | Form |
| ------------------- | ---- |
| I / You / We / They | do   |
| He / She / It       | does |

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type="example" fullWidth>

- I do my homework.
- We do exercise.
- She does her homework.
- He does the work.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Do as an Auxiliary Verb

Do and does are used to form questions and negative sentences in the present simple.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Questions' fullWidth>

Do/Does + subject + base verb?

- Do you like music?
- Do they speak English?
- Does he play football?
- Does she work here?

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Negative' fullWidth>

Subject + do/does + not + base verb

- I don't like coffee.
- They don't live here.
- He doesn't play tennis.
- She doesn't work here.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Do vs Does

Use do with I, you, we, and they.

Use does with he, she, and it.

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Avoid This Mistake' fullWidth>

After does or doesn't, use the base form of the verb.

- ❌ Does she likes music?
- ✅ Does she like music?
- ❌ He doesn't plays football.
- ✅ He doesn't play football.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Short Answers

Use do or does in short answers.

<LessonBlock type="example">

Do you like English?

- Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

Does he work here?

- Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.

</LessonBlock>
