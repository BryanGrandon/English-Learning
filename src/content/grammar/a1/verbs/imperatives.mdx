**Imperatives** are used to give **commands, instructions, advice, requests, or warnings**.

The imperative uses the **base form of the verb**. The subject **you** is understood and is usually omitted.

---

## Form

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Imperative Structure' fullWidth>

**Base verb + complement**

- Open the door.
- Sit down.
- Listen carefully.
- Read the sentence.

The subject **you** is not written.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Negative Imperatives

To make a negative imperative, use **don't** before the base verb.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title="Don't + Base Verb" fullWidth>

- Don't run.
- Don't touch that.
- Don't be late.
- Don't open the window.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Common Uses

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='When We Use Imperatives' fullWidth>

| Use         | Example               |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| Command     | Close the door.       |
| Instruction | Turn left.            |
| Request     | Please sit down.      |
| Advice      | Take a break.         |
| Warning     | Don't touch the fire. |

</LessonBlock>

---

## Please for Polite Requests

Use **please** to make an imperative more polite.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Polite Imperatives' fullWidth>

- Please open the window.
- Please help me.
- Please wait here.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Be in Imperatives

The verb **be** also has imperative forms.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Imperatives with Be' fullWidth>

- Be careful.
- Be quiet.
- Be kind.

Negative:

- Don't be noisy.
- Don't be afraid.

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title='Avoid This Mistake' fullWidth>

Use the **base form** of the verb after **don't**.

- ❌ Don't opens the door.
- ✅ Don't open the door.

- ❌ Don't goes there.
- ✅ Don't go there.

</LessonBlock>
