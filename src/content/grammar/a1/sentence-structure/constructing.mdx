English sentences usually follow a specific word order. Learning this order helps you build clear and correct sentences.

<LessonBlock type='rule' title='Subject + Verb + Object' >

- I play football.
- She reads books.
- They like music.

</LessonBlock>

Not every sentence needs an object. Some sentences only need a subject and a verb.

---

## Subject + Verb

The simplest English sentences can be formed with a subject + verb.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Verb' fullWidth>

- I work.
- She runs.
- They sleep.
- We study.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='example' title='Examples' fullWidth>

The subject tells us who or what performs the action.

The verb tells us what the subject does.

- Tom works.
- Birds fly.
- We study.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Subject + Verb + Object

When a verb acts on someone or something, we can add an object.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Verb + Object' >

- I read books.
- She drinks water.
- They play football.
- We watch movies.

</LessonBlock>

The object receives or is affected by the action of the verb.

---

## Affirmative Sentences

An affirmative sentence states that something is true or happens.

The basic structure is:

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Subject + Verb + Object' >

- I like pizza.
- He plays football.
- We study English.
- They watch TV.

</LessonBlock>

The object can be omitted when the verb does not need one:

- I work.
- She runs.
- They sleep.

---

## Negative Sentences

A negative sentence says that something is not true or does not happen.

With most verbs in the present simple, we use do not (don't) or does not (doesn't).

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Negative'>

Subject + do/does + not + base verb + object

- I don't like coffee.
- You don't play tennis.
- He doesn't like pizza.
- She doesn't watch TV.

</LessonBlock>

After doesn't, use the base form of the verb:

- She doesn't like coffee. ✅
- She doesn't likes coffee. ❌

---

## Questions

Questions usually change the word order.

With most verbs in the present simple, use do or does at the beginning.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Yes/No Questions'>

Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb + Object?

- Do you like pizza?
- Do they play football?
- Does he speak English?
- Does she work here?

</LessonBlock>

Remember that the main verb stays in its base form after does:

- Does she like music? ✅
- Does she likes music? ❌

---

## Short Answers

We often use short answers instead of repeating the complete sentence.

For yes/no questions, use do, does, don't, or doesn't.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Yes' fullWidth>

Yes, + subject + do/does.

- Do you like pizza?
  - → Yes, I do.
- Does she speak English?
  - → Yes, she does.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='No' fullWidth>

No, + subject + don't/doesn't.

- Do you like coffee?
  - → No, I don't.
- Does he play tennis?
  - → No, he doesn't.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

<LessonBlock type='important' title='Remember' fullWidth>

The auxiliary verb changes according to the subject:

- I / you / we / they → do / don't
- he / she / it → does / doesn't

</LessonBlock>

---

## Word Order

English generally follows a fixed word order. Changing the position of words can make a sentence incorrect or change its meaning.

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Basic Sentence Patterns'>

| Sentence type | Structure                               | Example                 |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Affirmative   | Subject + Verb + Object                 | She likes music.        |
| Negative      | Subject + do/does + not + Verb + Object | She doesn't like music. |
| Question      | Do/Does + Subject + Verb + Object?      | Does she like music?    |
| Short answer  | Yes/No + Subject + do/does              | Yes, she does.          |

</LessonBlock>

<LessonBlock type='tip' title='Build Your Sentence' fullWidth>

When constructing a basic sentence, think in this order:

1. Who? → 2. Does what? → 3. What/whom?

- She → reads → books.
- They → play → football.
- I → watch → movies.

</LessonBlock>
