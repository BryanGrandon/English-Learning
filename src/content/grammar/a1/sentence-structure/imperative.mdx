# Imperative Sentences

Imperative sentences are used to:

- Give orders
- Give instructions
- Make requests
- Give warnings

<Callout type="example">

- Open the door
- Don't touch that

</Callout>

The subject is **_not written_** because it is always "**you**"

---

## Affirmative Imperatives

Used to give orders or instructions

<Grammar type='structure' title='Affirmative Imperatives' highlight='Verb + complement'>

- #### Examples.
  - Open the door
  - Wash your hands
  - Tell me the truth

</Grammar>

---

## Negative Imperatives

Used to tell someone _NOT to do something_

<Grammar type='structure' title='Negative Imperatives' highlight={`Do not (don't) + verb + complement`}>

- #### Examples.
  - Do not lie
  - Don't run
  - Don't hit your sister

</Grammar>

---

## Using “Let's” (Suggestions)

Used when we include ourselves (**we**)

<Grammar type='structure' title={`Let's`} highlight={`Let's + verb`}>

- #### Examples.
  - Let's go
  - Let's study
  - Let's play football

</Grammar>

---

### Negative with Let's

<Grammar type='structure' title={`Let's not`} highlight={`Let's not + verb`}>

- #### Examples.
  - Let's not fight
  - Let's not argue

</Grammar>

---

## Important Notes

- Imperatives can sound _strong or direct_
- Use **_“please”_** to be polite
  - Please sit down
  - Please help me
- They often start directly with a **_verb_**

---

## Quick Summary

- No subject (it’s always “you”)
- Verb in base form
- Negative → use **_don’t_**
- “Let’s” → suggestions
