Imperatives are sentences we use to give orders, instructions, requests, or advice.

The subject is usually understood to be you, so we normally do not say it.

<LessonBlock type="structure" title="Base verb + complement">

- Open the door.
- Listen carefully.
- Sit down.

</LessonBlock>

---

## Affirmative Imperatives

An affirmative imperative tells someone to do something.

We use the base form of the verb without a subject.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Base verb + complement' >

- Open the window.
- Close the door.
- Read the book.
- Listen carefully.
- Come here.

</LessonBlock>

The verb does not change according to the person.

- Open the door. ✅
- Opens the door. ❌
- You open the door. → Possible in special contexts, but not the normal imperative form.

---

## Negative Imperatives

A negative imperative tells someone not to do something.

We use don't + base verb.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title="Don't + base verb + complement" >

- Don't open the door.
- Don't touch that.
- Don't run.
- Don't be late.
- Don't worry.

</LessonBlock>

### Don't + Verb

The verb after don't stays in the base form.

- Don't touch the screen. ✅
- Don't touches the screen. ❌

<LessonBlock type='common_mistake' title="Don't + Base Verb" >

Do not add -s, -ed, or -ing after don't.

- Don't run. ✅
- Don't runs. ❌
- Don't running. ❌

</LessonBlock>

---

## Please + Imperative

We can use please to make an imperative sound more polite.

Please can come at the beginning or at the end of the sentence.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Please + Imperative' fullWidth>

Please + base verb + complement

- Please sit down.
- Please open the window.
- Please wait here.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Imperative + Please' fullWidth>

Base verb + complement + please

- Sit down, please.
- Open the window, please.
- Wait here, please.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Uses of Imperatives

Imperatives are used in several common situations.

<ContentGrid>
<LessonBlock type='usage' title='Orders' fullWidth>

Use imperatives to tell someone what to do.

- Stop!
- Come here.
- Be quiet.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='usage' title='Instructions' fullWidth>

Use imperatives to give instructions.

- Turn left.
- Open the book.
- Write your name.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='usage' title='Requests' fullWidth>

Use please to make a request more polite.

- Please help me.
- Please wait here.
- Please close the door.

</LessonBlock>
<LessonBlock type='usage' title='Prohibitions' fullWidth>

Use don't + verb to tell someone not to do something.

- Don't touch that.
- Don't run.
- Don't open the door.

</LessonBlock>
</ContentGrid>

---

## Imperatives with Be

The verb be also has an imperative form.

<LessonBlock type='structure' title='Be + Complement' >

- Be careful.
- Be quiet.
- Be patient.
- Be polite.

For negative imperatives, use:

Don't be + complement

- Don't be late.
- Don't be noisy.
- Don't be afraid.

</LessonBlock>

Imperatives: Affirmative vs. Negative

<LessonBlock type='comparison' title='Compare' >

| Type            | Structure                       | Example                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Affirmative     | Base verb + complement          | Open the door.              |
| Negative        | Don't + base verb + complement  | Don't open the door.        |
| Polite          | Please + base verb + complement | Please open the door.       |
| Polite negative | Please don't + base verb        | Please don't open the door. |

</LessonBlock>
