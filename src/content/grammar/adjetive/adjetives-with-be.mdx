We can use the verb be with an adjective to describe a person, animal, place, or thing.

The basic structure is:

- #### Subject + am/is/are + adjective
  - I am happy.
  - She is tired.
  - They are ready.

---

## Am, Is, and Are + Adjective

The form of be depends on the subject.

| Subject | Be  | Example         |
| ------- | --- | --------------- |
| I       | am  | I am happy.     |
| You     | are | You are tired.  |
| He      | is  | He is ready.    |
| She     | is  | She is happy.   |
| It      | is  | It is cold.     |
| We      | are | We are ready.   |
| They    | are | They are tired. |

### Examples

- I am happy.
- You are kind.
- He is hungry.
- She is tired.
- It is cold.
- We are ready.
- They are busy.

---

## Affirmative Sentences

Use:

**_Subject + am/is/are + adjective_**

### Examples

- I am happy.
- She is tired.
- He is hungry.
- We are ready.
- They are busy.

The adjective describes the subject.

- #### In: She is tired.
  - she = subject
  - is = verb be
  - tired = adjective

---

## Negative Sentences

To make a negative sentence, add not after am, is, or are.

**_Subject + am/is/are + not + adjective_**

### Examples

- I am not tired.
- You are not late.
- He is not hungry.
- She is not busy.
- It is not cold.
- We are not ready.
- They are not happy.

### Contractions

In everyday English, we often use contractions.

| Full form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| I am not  | I'm not     |
| is not    | isn't       |
| are not   | aren't      |

- #### Examples:
  - I am not tired.
  - I'm not tired.
  - She is not busy.
  - She isn't busy.
  - They are not ready.
  - They aren't ready.

Note: We normally do not contract am not as amn't in standard English.

---

## Questions with Be

To make a basic question, put am, is, or are before the subject.

**_Am/Is/Are + subject + adjective?_**

### Examples

- Are you tired?
- Is she happy?
- Is he ready?
- Are they busy?
- Am I late?

---

## Short Answers

We can answer basic be + adjective questions with short answers.

### Yes

**_Yes + subject + am/is/are._**

- Are you tired?
  - Yes, I am.
- Is she happy?
  - Yes, she is.
- Are they ready?
  - Yes, they are.

### No

**_No + subject + am/is/are + not._**

- Are you tired?
  - No, I'm not.
- Is he hungry?
  - No, he isn't.
- Are they ready?
  - No, they aren't.

## Compare Affirmative, Negative, and Questions

The same idea can be expressed in three ways.

| Type        | Example          |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| Affirmative | She is tired.    |
| Negative    | She isn't tired. |
| Question    | Is she tired?    |

### Another example:

| Type        | Example            |
| ----------- | ------------------ |
| Affirmative | They are ready.    |
| Negative    | They aren't ready. |
| Question    | Are they ready?    |

## Quick Summary

- Use am, is, or are before an adjective.
- I + am → I am happy.
- He/She/It + is → She is tired.
- You/We/They + are → They are ready.
- For negatives, use not: She is not tired.
- For questions, put be before the subject: Are you tired?
- Short answers use be: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
