Adjectives can appear in different positions in a sentence. In basic English, adjectives are commonly used before a noun or after the verb be.

---

## Adjective + Noun

When an adjective directly describes a noun, the adjective usually comes before the noun.

- #### Adjective + noun
  - a big house
  - a small dog
  - a new phone
  - an old car
  - a good book
- #### Compare:
  - a big house

The adjective big describes the noun house.

---

## Adjective Before a Noun

The most common position for a basic adjective is before a noun.

- #### Examples
  - a red car
  - a beautiful flower
  - a small apartment
  - an old computer
  - a good student

The adjective comes directly before the noun it describes.

- She has a new phone.
- He lives in a small house.
- They have a good teacher.

### Word Order

The basic pattern is:

**_article + adjective + noun_**

| Article | Adjective | Noun  |
| ------- | --------- | ----- |
| a       | big       | house |
| a       | small     | dog   |
| a       | new       | phone |
| an      | old       | car   |
| a       | good      | book  |

---

## Multiple Adjectives

We can use more than one adjective to describe the same noun.

For basic descriptions, adjectives can appear together before the noun.

- a big red car
- a small black dog
- a beautiful old house
- a new blue phone
- a big green tree

### Examples in Sentences

- She has a small black dog.
- They live in a beautiful old house.
- I have a new blue phone.

The adjectives small and black both describe dog.

The adjectives beautiful and old both describe house.

---

## Adjectives After Be

Adjectives can also come after the verb be.

In this structure, the adjective describes the subject.

- #### Subject + be + adjective
  - The house is big.
  - The dog is small.
  - The phone is new.
  - The car is old.
  - The book is good.
- #### Examples
  - The house is big.
  - The dog is small.
  - My phone is new.
  - His car is old.

Here, big, small, new, and old describe the subject.

---

## Adjective + Noun vs. Be + Adjective

The same adjective can be used in both structures.

| Before a noun | After be          |
| ------------- | ----------------- |
| a big house   | The house is big. |
| a small dog   | The dog is small. |
| a new phone   | The phone is new. |
| an old car    | The car is old.   |
| a good book   | The book is good. |

Notice the difference in word order:

- #### Adjective + noun
  - a big house
- #### Subject + be + adjectives
  - The house is big.

---

## Common Sentence Patterns

### Pattern 1: Adjective + Noun

**_a/an + adjective + noun_**

- She has a new car.
- He has an old bike.

### Pattern 2: Subject + Be + Adjective

**_subject + am/is/are + adjective_**

- The car is new.
- The bike is old.

### Pattern 3: Multiple Adjectives + Noun

**_a/an + adjective + adjective + noun_**

- It is a big red car.
- She has a small black dog.

---

## Quick Summary

- An adjective usually comes before a noun when it directly describes the noun.
- The basic pattern is adjective + noun: a big house.
- We can use multiple adjectives before a noun: a big red car.
- An adjective can also come after be: The house is big.
- With be, the basic pattern is subject + be + adjective.
