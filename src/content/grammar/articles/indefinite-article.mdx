The indefinite articles a and an are used before singular countable nouns when we talk about a person, place, animal, or thing that is not specific or is mentioned for the first time.

---

## What Are the Indefinite Articles?

English has two indefinite articles:

- a
- an

Both have the same basic meaning. The choice depends on the sound that follows.

| Article | Use                      | Example  |
| ------- | ------------------------ | -------- |
| a       | Before a consonant sound | a book   |
| an      | Before a vowel sound     | an apple |

---

## When Do We Use "A" and "An"?

### 1. When We Talk About Something for the First Time

Use a/an when we introduce something for the first time.

- I saw a dog in the park.
- She bought a book.
- He found an old coin.

When we mention the same thing again, we usually use the.

- I saw a dog in the park.
- The dog was very friendly.

### 2. When Something Is Not Specific

Use a/an when we do not mean a particular person or thing.

- I need a pen.
- She wants a new phone.
- He is looking for an apartment.

The exact pen, phone, or apartment is not specified.

- #### Compare:
  - I need a pen. -> Any pen.
  - I need the pen on the table. -> A specific pen.

### 3. To Mean "One"

A/an can have a similar meaning to one when talking about a single thing.

- I have a brother.
- She bought an apple.
- He needs a ticket.

This means one brother, one apple, and one ticket.

### 4. To Describe Someone or Something

Use a/an before a singular countable noun when describing what someone or something is.

- She is a teacher.
- He is a student.
- It is an animal.
- This is a computer.

We use a/an because the noun refers to one member of a group or category.

---

## When Do We Use "A"?

Use a before a consonant sound.

- a book
- a car
- a dog
- a house
- a teacher
- a university

### Examples:

- I have a car.
- She is a teacher.
- He lives in a house.

---

## When Do We Use "An"?

Use an before a vowel sound.

- an apple
- an egg
- an elephant
- an idea
- an orange
- an umbrella

### Examples:

- I ate an apple.
- She has an idea.
- He bought an umbrella.

---

## A or An Depends on the Sound

The choice between a and an depends on the sound, not simply the first letter.

### Consonant Sound -> "A"

- a university -> /juː.../
- a European country -> /jʊə.../
- a useful book -> /juː.../

These words begin with a vowel letter, but their first sound is a consonant sound.

### Vowel Sound -> "An"

- an hour -> /aʊ.../
- an honest person -> /ɒ.../
- an umbrella -> /ʌ.../

These words begin with a consonant letter in some cases, but their first sound is a vowel sound.

---

## A/An With Jobs and Professions

Use a/an when talking about someone's job or profession.

- She is a doctor.
- He is a teacher.
- My brother is an engineer.
- She is an artist.

Do not usually omit the article.

- ❌ She is teacher.
- ✅ She is a teacher.
- ❌ He is engineer.
- ✅ He is an engineer.

---

## A/An With Singular Countable Nouns

A/an can only be used with singular countable nouns.

### Singular Countable

- a book
- a car
- an apple
- an animal

### Plural

Do not use a/an with plural nouns.

- ❌ a books
- ✅ books
- ❌ an apples
- ✅ apples

### Uncountable

Do not normally use a/an directly with uncountable nouns.

- ❌ a water
- ✅ water
- ❌ an information
- ✅ information

When we want to count an uncountable noun, we can use an appropriate unit or container.

- a bottle of water
- a glass of water
- a piece of information

---

## A/An vs. The

Use a/an when something is not specific or is introduced for the first time.

Use the when something is specific or already known.

| Article | Meaning                      | Example               |
| ------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------- |
| a/an    | Not specific / first mention | I saw a dog.          |
| the     | Specific / known             | The dog was friendly. |

### Example

- I bought a book yesterday.
- The book is about animals.

The first sentence introduces a book. The second sentence refers to that specific book.

---

## Common Mistakes

### Using "an" before every vowel letter

- ❌ an university
- ✅ a university

The word begins with the consonant sound /j/.

### Using "a" before a vowel sound

- ❌ a apple
- ✅ an apple

The word begins with a vowel sound.

### Using "a/an" with plural nouns

- ❌ a books
- ✅ books

### Using "a/an" with uncountable nouns

- ❌ an information
- ✅ information
- Or:
  - a piece of information

### Forgetting the article with jobs

- ❌ She is doctor.
- ✅ She is a doctor.

---

## Quick Summary

- English has two indefinite articles: a and an.
- Use a before a consonant sound.
- Use an before a vowel sound.
- Use a/an with singular countable nouns.
- Use a/an when something is not specific.
- Use a/an when mentioning something for the first time.
- Use a/an when describing someone's job or profession.
- The choice between a and an depends on the sound, not just the spelling.

### Compare

- I saw a dog. The dog was black.
- She is a teacher.
- He is an engineer.
- I need a pen.
- I need an umbrella.
