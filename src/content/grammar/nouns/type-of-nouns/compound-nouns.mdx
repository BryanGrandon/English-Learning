A compound noun is a noun made from two or more words that work together to form a new meaning.

- #### Examples
  - tooth + brush -> toothbrush
  - class + room -> classroom
  - bus + stop -> bus stop
  - mother + in + law -> mother-in-law

The individual words have their own meanings, but together they create a noun with a specific meaning.

---

## What Is a Compound Noun?

A compound noun combines two or more words to name a person, place, thing, or idea.

### Examples

<div>

| Words         | Compound Noun | Meaning                              |
| ------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| tooth + brush | toothbrush    | something used to clean your teeth   |
| class + room  | classroom     | a room where students study          |
| bed + room    | bedroom       | a room where you sleep               |
| rain + coat   | raincoat      | clothing that protects you from rain |
| bus + stop    | bus stop      | a place where buses stop             |

</div>

- #### Example in a Sentence
  - I need a new toothbrush.

The words tooth and brush combine to create the compound noun toothbrush.

---

## How Are Compound Nouns Formed?

Compound nouns can be formed in different ways.

### Noun + Noun

This is one of the most common patterns.

noun + noun → compound noun

- #### Examples:
  - bed + room → bedroom
  - school + bus → school bus
  - coffee + shop → coffee shop
  - tooth + paste → toothpaste
  - football + player → football player
- #### Examples in Sentences
  - My bedroom is upstairs.
  - I take the school bus every morning.
  - There is a coffee shop near my house.
  - I need some toothpaste.

---

## Adjective + Noun

Some compound nouns are formed with an adjective and a noun.

**_adjective + noun → compound noun_**

- #### Examples:
  - black + board → blackboard
  - green + house → greenhouse
  - full + moon → full moon
  - high + way → highway

The teacher wrote on the blackboard.

We drove on the highway.

---

## Verb + Noun

Some compound nouns combine a verb and a noun.

**_verb + noun → compound noun_**

- #### Examples:
  - swim + ming pool → swimming pool
  - washing + machine → washing machine
  - driving + school → driving school

The washing machine is in the kitchen.

She goes to a driving school.

---

## Three Ways to Write Compound Nouns

Compound nouns can be written in three main ways:

1. Closed compound nouns
2. Hyphenated compound nouns
3. Open compound nouns

---

### Closed Compound Nouns

A closed compound noun is written as one word.

- #### Examples
  - bedroom
  - classroom
  - toothbrush
  - toothpaste
  - football
  - airport
  - newspaper
  - sunflower
- #### Examples in Sentences
  - My bedroom is small.
  - The airport is busy.
  - I read the newspaper every morning.

---

### Hyphenated Compound Nouns

A hyphenated compound noun uses a hyphen (-) between the words.

#### Examples

- mother-in-law
- sister-in-law
- check-in
- runner-up
- self-control

#### Examples in Sentences

- My mother-in-law lives nearby.
- The check-in starts at 3 p.m.
- He won the runner-up position.

Not every combination of words uses a hyphen. The spelling of compound nouns depends on the word and standard usage.

---

### Open Compound Nouns

An open compound noun is written as two or more separate words.

#### Examples

- bus stop
- coffee shop
- swimming pool
- school bus
- post office
- living room
- dining room

Although the words are separate, they work together as one noun.

#### Examples in Sentences

- The bus stop is near my house.
- Let's meet at the coffee shop.
- The children are in the swimming pool.
- The post office closes at 5 p.m.

---

## Compound Nouns and Their Meaning

The meaning of a compound noun is not always exactly the same as the meanings of its individual words.

- #### For example:
  - butter + fly → butterfly

A butterfly is not a type of butter.

- #### Another example:
  - black + board → blackboard

A blackboard is a board used for writing, not simply a board that is black.

The combination creates a new meaning.

---

## Compound Nouns in Sentences

Compound nouns can have different roles in a sentence.

- #### As the Subjects
  - The bus stop is nearby.
- #### As the Objects
  - I need a new school bag.
- #### After "a" or "an"
  - She has a bedroom upstairs.
  - He works at an airport.
- #### With Plural Forms
- The plural form normally applies to the main noun.
  - one school bus -> two school buses
  - one coffee shop -> three coffee shops
  - one toothbrush -> two toothbrushes

---

## Plural Compound Nouns

For many compound nouns, we make the final word plural.

<div>

| Singular        | Plural           |
| --------------- | ---------------- |
| school bus      | school buses     |
| coffee shop     | coffee shops     |
| bedroom         | bedrooms         |
| toothbrush      | toothbrushes     |
| football player | football players |

</div>

### Examples

- There are two bus stops near here.
- The city has many coffee shops.
- Three football players are training.

Some compound nouns have different plural patterns, especially more complex or older expressions.

---

## Compound Nouns vs. Simple Nouns

A simple noun consists of one main word.

- book
- teacher
- house

A compound noun combines two or more words.

- textbook
- classroom
- greenhouse

### Compare

<div>

| Simple Noun | Compound Noun |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| room        | classroom     |
| book        | textbook      |
| house       | greenhouse    |
| board       | blackboard    |
| airport     | airport       |

</div>

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Treating a compound noun as unrelated words

- ❌ I left my tooth and brush in the bathroom.

If you mean the object used to clean your teeth, use:

- ✅ I left my toothbrush in the bathroom.

### 2. Incorrect pluralization

For most open compound nouns, make the final noun plural.

- ❌ two school buss
- ✅ two school buses
- ❌ three coffee shop
- ✅ three coffee shops

### 3. Confusing open and closed forms

Some compound nouns are written together:

- bedroom

Others are written separately:

- living room

The spelling must be learned with the compound noun because there is no single rule that determines whether every compound is open or closed.

A compound noun combines two or more words to create a noun with a specific meaning.

Compound nouns can be closed, hyphenated, or open.
