Nouns can be countable or uncountable depending on whether we can count them as individual things.

---

## What Are Countable Nouns?

Countable nouns are nouns for things we can _count using numbers_. They have both _singular and plural forms_.
We use countable nouns for **people, animals, objects, and other things** that we can count.

### Singular Form

We use a singular countable noun to talk about one person, animal, object, or thing.

- #### Rule
  - **_A, An or One_**

A singular countable noun can be used with **_a_**, **_an_**, or the **_number one_**.

- a book
- an umbrella
- one book

---

### Plural Form

We use plural countable nouns to talk about _two or more_ people, animals, objects, or things.
Plural countable nouns can be _used with numbers, many, and other plural determiners_.

- #### Example
  - She has **three dogs**.
  - I own **a house**.
  - **How many friends** do you have?

---

## Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns are things that we usually think of as a **substance**, **material**, **idea**, or **quantity** rather than individual objects.

Uncountable nouns usually _do not have a plural form_ and are used with a **singular verb**.

### Common Examples

<div>

| Category  | Uncountable Nouns              |
| --------- | ------------------------------ |
| Food      | rice, bread, sugar, cheese     |
| Drinks    | water, milk, coffee, tea       |
| Materials | wood, paper, glass             |
| Ideas     | information, advice, knowledge |
| Other     | money, time, homework          |

</div>

- #### Examples
  - I **drink** water every day.
  - She needs **some** information.
  - We have **bread** for breakfast.
  - He gave me **good** advice.
  - I don't have **much** money.

---

### Expressing Quantity

To express a quantity, use words or expressions such as:

- **some** water
- **a lot of** money
- **much** information
- **a bit of** advice
- **a cup of** tea
- **1 kg of** rice
- **a pinch of** salt

We don't normally use **_a/an_** directly with an **uncountable noun**.

---

## Countable vs. Uncountable

Some nouns describe individual objects, while others describe substances, concepts, or things viewed as a whole.

<div>

| Countable | Uncountable |
| --------- | ----------- |
| apple     | water       |
| book      | rice        |
| chair     | milk        |
| car       | money       |
| student   | information |
| egg       | bread       |

</div>

### Compare

- #### Countable:
  - I have three apples.
- #### Uncountable:
  - I have some rice.

We can count the apples individually, but we normally don't count rice as individual units.

---

### Examples

- I have two apples.
- I have some rice.
- She bought three books.
- She gave me some advice.

---

## Quantifiers

Different quantifiers are used with countable and uncountable nouns.

<div>

| Countable | Uncountable     | Both     |
| --------- | --------------- | -------- |
| many      | much            | some     |
| few       | little          | any      |
| a few     | a little        | a lot of |
| several   | a great deal of | lots of  |

</div>

### Countable nouns

Use many, few, a few, and several with plural countable nouns.

- There are many students here.
- I have a few books.
- She has several friends.

---

### Uncountable nouns

Use much, little, and a little with uncountable nouns.

- We don't have much time.
- I have a little money.
- There is little water left.

---

### Both

Use some, any, a lot of, and lots of with both types.

- I have some books.
- I have some money.
- There are a lot of people here.
- There is a lot of water here.

---

## Quantifiers Table

<div>

| Quantifier         | Main Use                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| many               | Questions and negative sentences            |
| much               | Questions and negative sentences            |
| some               | Affirmative sentences, offers, and requests |
| any                | Questions and negative sentences            |
| no / none          | Express zero quantity                       |
| a lot of / lots of | Informal alternative to _many_ and _much_   |
| few / a few        | Express a small number                      |

</div>

## Quick Summary

| Feature                     | Countable | Uncountable |
| --------------------------- | --------- | ----------- |
| Can be counted individually | ✅        | ❌          |
| Singular form               | ✅        | Usually ❌  |
| Plural form                 | ✅        | Usually ❌  |
| Use numbers directly        | ✅        | ❌          |
| many                        | ✅        | ❌          |
| much                        | ❌        | ✅          |
| some                        | ✅        | ✅          |
| any                         | ✅        | ✅          |
| a lot of                    | ✅        | ✅          |
| How many...?                | ✅        | ❌          |
| How much...?                | ❌        | ✅          |

### Remember

- #### Countable nouns can be counted:
  - one apple → two apples
- #### Uncountable nouns are not normally counted individually:
  - water, rice, money, information
- #### When you need to count an uncountable noun, use a unit or container:
  - a bottle of water
  - two pieces of information
  - three cups of coffee
