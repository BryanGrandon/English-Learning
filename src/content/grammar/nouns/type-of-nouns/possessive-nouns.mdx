Possessive nouns show that something belongs to someone or something.

---

## What is a Possessive Noun?

A possessive noun tells us who owns or has something.

We usually use an **apostrophe (')** with s to form possessive nouns.

- Sarah's book → the book belongs to Sarah.
- John's car → the car belongs to John.
- the dog's tail → the tail belongs to the dog.

### Basic Pattern

**_Possessor + 's + thing_**

- #### Maria's phone
  - Maria → possessor
  - 's → shows possession
  - phone → thing that belongs to Maria

---

## Singular Nouns

For a singular noun, add **_'s_**.

<div>

| Noun    | Possessive |
| ------- | ---------- |
| girl    | girl's     |
| boy     | boy's      |
| teacher | teacher's  |
| dog     | dog's      |
| James   | James's    |

</div>

- #### Example
  - This is the girl's bag.
  - That is the teacher's desk.
  - I like Tom's car.
  - The dog's name is Max.

---

## Plural Nouns Ending in -s

For regular plural nouns that already end in -s, add _only_ an **apostrophe (')**.

<div>

| Plural Nouns | Possessive |
| ------------ | ---------- |
| girls        | girls'     |
| boys         | boys'      |
| teachers     | teachers'  |
| students     | students'  |
| parents      | parents'   |

</div>

- #### Example
  - The girls' classroom is upstairs.
  - The students' books are on the table.
  - The teachers' office is next to the library.
  - My parents' house is in Chile.

---

## Irregular Plural Nouns

Some plural nouns do not end in -s. For these nouns, use 's.

<div>

| Plural Noun | Possessive |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| children    | children's |
| men         | men's      |
| women       | women's    |
| people      | people's   |

</div>

- #### Examples
  - The children's toys are here.
  - The women's bathroom is over there.
  - The men's clothes are on the second floor.
  - These are people's opinions.

---

## Possessive Nouns with Names

We can use possessive nouns with people's names.

### One Person

- #### Use 's.
  - Anna's phone
  - David's house
  - Peter's dog

---

### More Than One Person

If the plural name ends in -s, use an apostrophe.

- the Smiths' house
- the Browns' car

---

## Asking About Possession

We can use Whose...? to ask who something belongs to.

- #### Pattern
  - **_Whose + noun + is/are + ...?_**
- #### Examples
  - Whose book is this?
    - It's Maria's book.
  - Whose phone is that?s
    - It's John's phone.
  - Whose shoes are these?
    - They are Tom's shoes.
  - Whose bags are those?
    - They are the students' bags.

---

## Possessive Nouns vs. Possessive Adjectives

Possessive nouns and possessive adjectives both show possession, but they are formed differently.

<div>

| Possessive Noun         | Possessive Adjective |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| Sarah's book            | her book             |
| John's car              | his car              |
| the dog's food          | its food             |
| the students' classroom | their classroom      |

</div>

- #### Examples
  - Sarah's book is new.
  - Her book is new.

Both sentences refer to the same relationship.

---

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ Don't use an apostrophe to make a regular plural

- #### Incorrect:
  - two dog's
- #### Correct:
  - two dogs

The apostrophe is used to show possession, not simply to make a noun plural.

---

### ❌ Don't confuse plural and possessive forms

<div>

| Plural   | Possessive            |
| -------- | --------------------- |
| dogs     | dog's                 |
| girls    | girl's / girls'       |
| students | student's / students' |

</div>

- #### Compare:
  - The dogs are outside. → plural
  - The dog's food is here. → possession
  - The students are studying. → plural
  - The students' books are open. → possession

---

## Quick Summary

<div>

| Rule                         | Example             |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Singular noun + 's           | the girl's bag      |
| Plural noun ending in -s + ' | the girls' bags     |
| Irregular plural + 's        | the children's toys |

</div>

Ask about possession with Whose...? Whose book is this?

### Remember

Possessive nouns show who something belongs to.

- the boy's bike → one boy
- the boys' bikes → more than one boy
- the children's toys → irregular plural
