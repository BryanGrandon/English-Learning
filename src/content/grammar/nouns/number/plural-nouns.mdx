Plural nouns refer to more than one person, animal, place, thing, or idea.

In English, most plural nouns are formed by adding -s or -es to the singular noun.

---

## Regular Plurals

Most nouns form the plural by adding -s.

| Singular | Plural   |
| -------- | -------- |
| book     | books    |
| car      | cars     |
| dog      | dogs     |
| house    | houses   |
| teacher  | teachers |

### Examples

- I have one book.
- I have two books.
- There is a car outside.
- There are three cars outside.

---

## Nouns Ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, and -z

Nouns ending in certain sounds and spellings usually form the plural with -es.

### Nouns Ending in -s

Many nouns ending in -s form their plural with -es.

- bus -> buses
- gas -> gases

There are two buses outside.

### Nouns Ending in -ss

Nouns ending in -ss form their plural by adding -es.

- class -> classes
- glass -> glasses

There are three classes today.

### Nouns Ending in -sh

Nouns ending in -sh form their plural by adding -es.

- dish -> dishes
- brush -> brushes

I washed the dishes.

### Nouns Ending in -ch

Nouns ending in -ch commonly form their plural by adding -es.

- watch -> watches
- sandwich -> sandwiches

She has two watches.

### Nouns Ending in -x

Nouns ending in -x form their plural by adding -es.

- box -> boxes
- fox -> foxes

There are two boxes on the table.

### Nouns Ending in -z

Nouns ending in -z commonly form their plural with -es.

- quiz -> quizzes

We have two quizzes today.

---

## Nouns Ending in -y

The plural depends on the letter before -y.

### Consonant + y

When a noun ends in a consonant + y, change -y to -ies.

**_consonant + y -> -ies_**

- city -> cities
- baby -> babies
- country -> countries
- family -> families

There are many cities in this country.

### Vowel + y

When a noun ends in a vowel + y, simply add -s.

vowel + y -> -ys

- boy -> boys
- toy -> toys
- day -> days
- key -> keys

The children have many toys.

### Quick Rule

| Ending        | Plural Rule | Example        |
| ------------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| consonant + y | -y -> -ies  | city -> cities |
| vowel + y     | add -s      | boy -> boys    |

---

## Pronunciation of Plural -s and -es

The plural ending -s / -es can have three different pronunciations.

### /s/

Use /s/ after a voiceless sound such as /p/, /t/, /k/, /f/.

- cat -> cats
- book -> books
- cup -> cups

### /z/

Use /z/ after a voiced sound such as a vowel or /b/, /d/, /g/, /m/, /n/, /l/, /r/.

- dog -> dogs
- car -> cars
- room -> rooms
- day -> days

### /ɪz/

Use /ɪz/ when the plural ending is added after a sound such as /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/.

- bus -> buses
- class -> classes
- dish -> dishes
- swatch -> watches
- quiz -> quizzes

### Quick Summary

| Ending | Sound | Example        |
| ------ | ----- | -------------- |
| -s     | /s/   | cats, books    |
| -s     | /z/   | dogs, cars     |
| -es    | /ɪz/  | buses, watches |

The pronunciation depends on the final sound of the singular noun, not simply on its spelling.

---

## Quick Summary

- Most nouns form the plural with -s: book -> books.
- Nouns ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -z usually take -es: box -> boxes.
- A consonant + -y changes to -ies: city -> cities.
- A vowel + -y takes -s: boy -> boys.
- Plural -s / -es can be pronounced /s/, /z/, or /ɪz/.
- Irregular plural forms follow different patterns and are studied separately.
