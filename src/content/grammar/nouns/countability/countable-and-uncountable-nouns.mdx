Nouns can be countable or uncountable depending on whether we can count them as individual things.

---

## What Are Countable Nouns?

Countable nouns are nouns for things we can _count using numbers_. They have both _singular and plural forms_.
We use countable nouns for **people, animals, objects, and other things** that we can count.

### Singular Form

We use a singular countable noun to talk about one person, animal, object, or thing.

- #### Rule
  - **_A, An or One_**

A singular countable noun can be used with **_a_**, **_an_**, or the **_number one_**.

- a book
- an umbrella
- one book

---

### Plural Form

We use plural countable nouns to talk about _two or more_ people, animals, objects, or things.
Plural countable nouns can be _used with numbers, many, and other plural determiners_.

- #### Example
  - She has **three dogs**.
  - I own **a house**.
  - **How many friends** do you have?

---

## Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns are things that we usually think of as a **substance**, **material**, **idea**, or **quantity** rather than individual objects.

Uncountable nouns usually _do not have a plural form_ and are used with a **singular verb**.

### Common Examples

<div>

| Category  | Uncountable Nouns              |
| --------- | ------------------------------ |
| Food      | rice, bread, sugar, cheese     |
| Drinks    | water, milk, coffee, tea       |
| Materials | wood, paper, glass             |
| Ideas     | information, advice, knowledge |
| Other     | money, time, homework          |

</div>

- #### Examples
  - I **drink** water every day.
  - She needs **some** information.
  - We have **bread** for breakfast.
  - He gave me **good** advice.
  - I don't have **much** money.

---

### Expressing Quantity

To express a quantity, use words or expressions such as:

- **some** water
- **a lot of** money
- **much** information
- **a bit of** advice
- **a cup of** tea
- **1 kg of** rice
- **a pinch of** salt

We don't normally use **_a/an_** directly with an **uncountable noun**.

---

## Countable vs. Uncountable

Some nouns describe individual objects, while others describe substances, concepts, or things viewed as a whole.

<div>

| Countable | Uncountable |
| --------- | ----------- |
| apple     | water       |
| book      | rice        |
| chair     | milk        |
| car       | money       |
| student   | information |
| egg       | bread       |

</div>

### Compare

- #### Countable:
  - I have three apples.
- #### Uncountable:
  - I have some rice.

We can count the apples individually, but we normally don't count rice as individual units.

---

### Examples

- I have two apples.
- I have some rice.
- She bought three books.
- She gave me some advice.

---

## Quick Summary

| Feature                          | Countable | Uncountable |
| -------------------------------- | --------- | ----------- |
| Can be counted individually      | ✅        | ❌          |
| Singular form                    | ✅        | Usually ❌  |
| Plural form                      | ✅        | Usually ❌  |
| Use numbers directly             | ✅        | ❌          |
| Singular countable uses **a/an** | ✅        | ❌          |
| Usually takes a singular verb    | ❌        | ✅          |

### Remember

- **Countable nouns** refer to individual things that can be counted: **one apple -> two apples**.
- **Uncountable nouns** refer to substances, materials, ideas, or things viewed as a whole: **water, rice, money, information**.
- Countable nouns can have **singular and plural forms**.
- Uncountable nouns usually have _no plural form_ and take a **singular verb**.
- To count an uncountable noun, use a _unit or container_:
  - _a bottle of water_
  - _two pieces of information_
  - _three cups of coffee_
