Possessive nouns show that something belongs to someone or something.

They usually answer the question “Whose?”

- #### For example:
  - Anna's book -> the book belongs to Anna.
  - the dog's toy -> the toy belongs to the dog.
  - my parents' house -> the house belongs to my parents.

---

## What is a Possessive Noun?

A possessive noun is a noun that shows ownership, belonging, or relationship with another noun.

| Regular Noun | Possessive Noun |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| Anna         | Anna's          |
| the dog      | the dog's       |
| my parents   | my parents'     |

The possessive noun usually comes before the noun it describes.

- Sarah's car is new.

Here, Sarah's tells us whose car it is.

---

## How Possessive Nouns Are Formed

Possessive nouns are commonly formed using 's or '.

### Singular Nouns

Add 's to most singular nouns.

- the boy's bike
- the teacher's desk
- the cat's food
- James's phone

### Plural Nouns Ending in -s

Add only an apostrophe (').

- the students' books
- my parents' house
- the girls' room
- the teachers' office

### Irregular Plural Nouns

Add 's to irregular plural nouns.

- children's toys
- men's clothes
- women's shoes
- people's opinions

---

## Possession and Relationships

Possessive nouns do not always describe physical ownership.

They can also describe relationships or associations.

- Maria's mother
- John's brother
- the company's employees
- the country's history

### Examples

- This is Tom's computer.
- I found the girl's phone.
- My parents' car is outside.
- The children's teacher is very friendly.
- We visited the company's office.

## Quick Summary

| Form          | Use                         | Example             |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- |
| noun's        | Singular noun               | the girl's bag      |
| plural noun'  | Regular plural ending in -s | the girls' bags     |
| plural noun's | Irregular plural            | the children's bags |

Possessive nouns show who or what something belongs to and usually come before the noun they describe.
