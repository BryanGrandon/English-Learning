The verb **be** is one of the most important verbs in English. In the Present Simple, it has three forms:

- **am**
- **is**
- **are**

We use **be** to talk about **people, things, descriptions, locations, feelings, and states**.

---

## Forms of Be

The form of **be** depends on the subject.

| Subject | Be  | Example           |
| ------- | --- | ----------------- |
| I       | am  | I am a student.   |
| You     | are | You are happy.    |
| He      | is  | He is tall.       |
| She     | is  | She is tired.     |
| It      | is  | It is cold.       |
| We      | are | We are friends.   |
| They    | are | They are at home. |

### Basic Pattern

- #### I -> am
  - I am happy.
- #### He / She / It -> is
  - He is tired.
  - She is a teacher.
  - It is cold.
- #### You / We / They -> are
  - You are ready.
  - We are friends.
  - They are students.

---

## Affirmative Sentences

The basic structure is:

_**Subject + am/is/are + complement**_

- #### Examples:
  - I am a student.
  - You are my friend.
  - He is a doctor.
  - She is happy.
  - It is cold.
  - We are ready.
  - They are at home.

The complement can be a **noun, adjective, place, or other information**.

- #### Noun
  - She is a teacher.
  - He is my brother.
- #### Adjective
  - I am tired.
  - They are happy.
- #### Place
  - We are at home.
  - She is at school.

---

## Contractions

In everyday English, **be** is often contracted.

| Full Form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| I am      | I'm         |
| You are   | You're      |
| He is     | He's        |
| She is    | She's       |
| It is     | It's        |
| We are    | We're       |
| They are  | They're     |

### Examples:

- I am happy. -> **I'm happy.**
- She is a student. -> **She's a student.**
- They are ready. -> **They're ready.**
- We are at home. -> **We're at home.**

Both forms are correct. The contracted form is very common in everyday English.

---

## Negative Sentences

To make a negative sentence, add **not** after the verb **be**.

**Subject + am/is/are + not + complement**

- #### Examples:
  - I am not tired.
  - You are not late.
  - He is not a teacher.
  - She is not happy.
  - It is not cold.
  - We are not ready.
  - They are not at home.

### Negative Contractions

| Full Form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| is not    | isn't       |
| are not   | aren't      |

- #### Examples:
  - She is not tired. -> **She isn't tired.**
  - He is not at home. -> **He isn't at home.**
  - We are not ready. -> **We aren't ready.**
  - They are not students. -> **They aren't students.**

With **I**, the usual negative form is:

**I am not -> I'm not**

- I'm not tired.
- I'm not a teacher.

---

## Questions

With **be**, we make questions by putting **am, is, or are before the subject**.

**Am/Is/Are + subject + complement?**

- #### Examples:
  - Am I late?
  - Are you a student?
  - Is he your brother?
  - Is she happy?
  - Is it cold?
  - Are we ready?
  - Are they at home?

Notice that we do **not** use **do/does** with **be**.

- ❌ Does she be happy?
- ✅ Is she happy?

---

## Short Answers

Short answers use **am, is, or are**.

### Positive

- Are you a student? -> **Yes, I am.**
- Is he your brother? -> **Yes, he is.**
- Is she tired? -> **Yes, she is.**
- Are they ready? -> **Yes, they are.**

### Negative

- Are you tired? -> **No, I'm not.**
- Is he a teacher? -> **No, he isn't.**
- Is she at home? -> **No, she isn't.**
- Are they ready? -> **No, they aren't.**

## Uses of Be

### Identity

Use **be** to say who someone is.

- I am Bryan.
- She is Maria.
- He is my brother.

### Occupations

- I am a student.
- She is a teacher.
- He is a doctor.

### Descriptions

Use **be** with adjectives to describe people or things.

- He is tall.
- She is beautiful.
- The house is big.
- They are happy.

### Feelings and States

- I am tired.
- She is hungry.
- We are ready.
- They are nervous.

### Location

- I am at home.
- She is at school.
- The books are on the table.
- They are in the park.

### Age

In English, we use **be** to talk about age.

- I am 20 years old.
- She is 18 years old.
- They are 10 years old.

We do **not** use **have** for age.

- ❌ I have 20 years old.
- ✅ I am 20 years old.

---

## Be + Adjective

A common pattern is:

**Subject + be + adjective**

- #### Examples:
  - I am happy.
  - You are tired.
  - He is tall.
  - She is hungry.
  - It is cold.
  - We are ready.
  - They are busy.

The adjective describes the subject.

---

## Be + Noun

We can use **be** before a noun to identify someone or something.

**Subject + be + noun**

- #### Examples:
  - I am a student.
  - She is a teacher.
  - He is my friend.
  - They are students.
  - This is a book.

With singular countable nouns, we normally use **a/an**.

- She is **a** teacher.
- He is **an** engineer.

---

## Be + Place

We can use **be** to say where someone or something is.

**Subject + be + place**

- #### Examples:
  - I am at home.
  - She is at school.
  - He is in the kitchen.
  - We are in Chile.
  - They are at the park.
  - The keys are on the table.

---

## Wh- Questions with Be

We can use question words with **be**.

- #### Common question words:
  - **What**
  - **Who**
  - **Where**
  - **How**

### Structure

**Wh-word + am/is/are + subject?**

- #### Examples:
  - What is your name?
  - Who is she?
  - Where are you?
  - Where is he?
  - How are you?
  - What are they?

### Examples

- **What is your name?**
  - -> My name is Bryan.
- **Where are you?**
  - -> I am at home.
- **How are you?**
  - -> I am fine.

---

## Common Mistakes

### Using the wrong form of be

- ❌ I is happy.
- ✅ I am happy.
- ❌ They is tired.
- ✅ They are tired.

### Forgetting be

- ❌ She happy.
- ✅ She is happy.

### Using do/does with be

- ❌ Does he be tired?
- ✅ Is he tired?

### Incorrect negative

- ❌ She don't happy.
- ✅ She isn't happy.

### Incorrect age expression

- ❌ I have 20 years old.
- ✅ I am 20 years old.
