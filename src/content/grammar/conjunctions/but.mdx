**But** is a conjunction used to connect two ideas that are different or contrasting.

It introduces a contrast.

---

## Connecting Ideas

- I like coffee, **but** I don't like tea.
- She is tired, **but** she is happy.
- He is young, **but** he is very responsible.
- The house is small, **but** it is comfortable.

The second idea is different from or contrasts with the first idea.

---

## Connecting Adjectives

We can use **but** to connect contrasting descriptions.

- The test is difficult **but** interesting.
- The restaurant is expensive **but** good.
- He is quiet **but** friendly.

---

## Connecting Sentences

**But** can connect two complete ideas.

- I want to go, **but** I am busy.
- She studied a lot, **but** she didn't pass the test.
- We have a car, **but** we don't use it often.

---

## But vs. And

The meaning changes depending on the conjunction.

### And

> I am tired, **and** I am hungry.

Both ideas are added.

### But

> I am tired, **but** I am hungry.

The ideas are presented as a contrast.

---

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ I like coffee **but** I like tea.

This is possible, but if there is no contrast, **and** is usually more appropriate.

- ✅ I like coffee **and** tea.
- ❌ I am tired, **but** I go to bed.

The relationship between the ideas is unclear.

Depending on the intended meaning:

- ✅ I am tired, **so** I go to bed.

## Quick Summary

- **But** introduces a contrast.
- It connects different or contrasting ideas.
- It is similar to **pero** in Spanish.
- Think: **A, but B** = B is different from A.
