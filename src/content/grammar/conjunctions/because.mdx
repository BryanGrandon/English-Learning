**Because** is a conjunction used to give a reason or explanation.

It answers the question **Why?**

---

## Giving a Reason

- I am tired **because** I worked all day.
- She is happy **because** she passed the test.
- We are staying home **because** it is raining.
- He is late **because** the bus is slow.

The part after **because** explains the reason.

> **result + because + reason**

---

## Because at the Beginning

We can also put the reason first.

> **Because + reason, + result**

- **Because it is raining,** we are staying home.
- **Because I am tired,** I am going to bed.
- **Because she is sick,** she isn't at school.

The meaning is the same.

- #### Compare:
  - > We are staying home **because it is raining**.
  - > **Because it is raining,** we are staying home.

---

## Answering "Why?"

We often use **because** to answer questions with **Why?**

**Why are you tired?**

> Because I worked all day.

**Why is she at home?**

> Because she is sick.

**Why are they late?**

> Because the bus is late.

---

## Because vs. And

**And** adds information.

> I am tired **and** I am hungry.

**Because** gives a reason.

> I am tired **because** I worked all day.

---

## Because vs. But

**Because** gives a reason.

> I am staying home **because** I am sick.

**But** introduces a contrast.

> I am sick, **but** I am going to work.

---

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ I am tired **because I worked**.
- ✅ I am tired **because I worked**.

This sentence is correct.

A common mistake is using **because** when there is no reason:

- ❌ I like coffee **because** I like tea.

If the intended meaning is simply to add another preference:

- ✅ I like coffee **and** tea.

---

## Quick Summary

- **Because** gives a reason or explanation.
- It answers **Why?**
- It can appear in the middle or at the beginning of a sentence.
- It is similar to **porque** in Spanish.
- Think: **result because reason**.
