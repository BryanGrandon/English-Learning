**Or** is a conjunction used to give a choice or alternative.

It presents two or more possibilities.

---

## Giving a Choice

- Do you want **tea or coffee**?
- Do you want **red or blue**?
- Is your name **John or James**?
- Would you like **pizza or pasta**?

The listener chooses one of the options.

---

## Connecting Verbs

**Or** can connect different actions.

- We can **walk or take** the bus.
- You can **stay or leave**.
- Do you want to **eat or drink**?

---

## Connecting Sentences

**Or** can connect two possible ideas.

- We can go to the park, **or** we can stay home.
- You can call me, **or** you can send me a message.
- We can study now, **or** we can study later.

---

## Questions with Or

**Or** is especially common in questions when giving alternatives.

- Do you want tea **or** coffee?
- Are you from Chile **or** Argentina?
- Is the meeting on Monday **or** Tuesday?

---

## And vs. Or

The meaning changes depending on the conjunction.

### And

> Do you want tea **and** coffee?

The speaker asks about both.

### Or

> Do you want tea **or** coffee?

The speaker gives a choice between the two.

---

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Do you want tea **and** coffee?

If you intend to offer a choice between them:

- ✅ Do you want tea **or** coffee?
- ❌ You can take the bus **or** walk.

This is understandable, but keeping the structure parallel is clearer:

- ✅ You can **take the bus or walk**.

---

## Quick Summary

- **Or** gives a choice or alternative.
- It connects two or more possibilities.
- It is similar to **o** in Spanish.
- Think: **A or B** = choose between alternatives.
