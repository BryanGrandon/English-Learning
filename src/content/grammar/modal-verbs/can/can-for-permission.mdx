We use **can** to ask for or give permission.

Permission means that someone says it is okay to do something.

---

## Asking for Permission

The basic structure is:

> **Can + subject + base verb?**

- **Can I sit** here?
- **Can I use** your phone?
- **Can we go** outside?
- **Can I open** the window?
- **Can they come** with us?

These questions ask if something is allowed.

---

## Giving Permission

We can use **can** to give permission.

- You **can sit** here.
- You **can use** my computer.
- You **can go** outside.
- You **can take** one.
- You **can come** with us.

### Common Responses

> **Yes, you can.**
> **Sure.**
> **Of course.**
> **No problem.**

---

## Refusing Permission

We can use **can't** when something is not allowed.

- You **can't park** here.
- You **can't use** this computer.
- You **can't enter** this room.
- You **can't take** photos here.

Here, **can't** means that something is not permitted.

---

## Ability vs. Permission

**Can** can have different meanings depending on the context.

### Ability

> I **can swim**.

This means I am able to swim.

### Permission

> You **can swim** here.

This means swimming is allowed here.

- #### Compare:
  - **Can you drive?** -> Are you able to drive?
  - **Can I drive?** -> Am I allowed to drive?

---

## Can + Base Verb

After **can**, always use the base form of the verb.

- ❌ Can I **to sit** here?
- ✅ Can I **sit** here?
- ❌ You can **uses** this.
- ✅ You can **use** this.

---

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Can I **to go** outside?
- ✅ Can I **go** outside?
- ❌ You can **goes** now.
- ✅ You can **go** now.
- ❌ You don't can park here.
- ✅ You **can't** park here.

---

## Quick Summary

- **Can** can ask for permission.
- **Can** can give permission.
- **Can't** can say that something is not allowed.
- Use **can + base verb**.
- The meaning depends on the context.
