Negative sentences are used to say that something is **not true**, **does not happen**, or **does not exist**.

In English, negative sentences commonly use **not** with an auxiliary verb.

---

## Negative Sentences with Be

With **be**, put **not** after the verb.

Structure:

- **Subject + be + not + ...**

Examples:

- I am not tired.
- She is not a teacher.
- They are not at home.

### Contractions

In everyday English, negative forms are often contracted.

| Full form | Contraction |
| --------- | ----------- |
| am not    | I'm not     |
| is not    | isn't       |
| are not   | aren't      |

- #### Examples:
  - I am not hungry.
  - I'm not hungry.
  - He is not here.
  - He isn't here.
  - We are not ready.
  - We aren't ready.

---

## Negative Sentences with Do

With most verbs in the present simple, use **do not** or **don't**.

Use **do not** with:

- I
- you
- we
- they

Structure:

- **Subject + do not + base verb**

Examples:

- I don't like coffee.
- You don't need help.
- We don't live here.
- They don't speak Spanish.

---

## Negative Sentences with Does

Use **does not** or **doesn't** with:

- he
- she
- it

Structure:

- **Subject + does not + base verb**

- #### Examples:
  - He doesn't work here.
  - She doesn't like coffee.
  - It doesn't work.

The main verb stays in the **base form**.

- She doesn't **like** tea. ✓
- She doesn't **likes** tea. ✗

### Why?

The **-s** moves from the main verb to **does**.

- She likes coffee.

becomes:

- She doesn't like coffee.

---

## Negative Sentences with Can

Use **cannot** or **can't** after the subject.

Structure:

- **Subject + can't + base verb**

Examples:

- I can't swim.
- She can't drive.
- We can't come today.
- They can't speak English.

---

## Negative Sentences with Have

When **have** is the main verb in the present simple, use **do/does** for negatives.

- I don't have a car.
- She doesn't have a brother.
- They don't have enough time.

With **have got**, use **not** with **have/has**.

- I haven't got a car.
- He hasn't got a car.

---

## Common Mistakes

### Don't use two negative auxiliaries

- I don't like coffee. ✓
- I don't not like coffee. ✗

### Don't add -s after doesn't

- He doesn't work here. ✓
- He doesn't works here. ✗

### Don't use do/does with be

- She isn't happy. ✓
- She doesn't be happy. ✗

---

## Negative vs. Affirmative

| Affirmative     | Negative              |
| --------------- | --------------------- |
| I am tired.     | I am not tired.       |
| She is happy.   | She isn't happy.      |
| They are ready. | They aren't ready.    |
| I like coffee.  | I don't like coffee.  |
| He works here.  | He doesn't work here. |
| We can swim.    | We can't swim.        |

---

## Quick Summary

- **Be:** subject + be + not
- **Present Simple:** subject + don't/doesn't + base verb
- **Can:** subject + can't + base verb
- After **doesn't**, always use the **base form** of the main verb.

### Remember

- She isn't tired.
- They don't live here.
- He doesn't speak English.
- I can't swim.
