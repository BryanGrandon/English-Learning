# Possessive 's

## What Is the Possessive 's?

The **possessive 's** is used to show that something belongs to someone or is connected to someone.

### Basic Structure

- **Person + 's + thing**

Examples:

- Maria's book
- John's car
- Sarah's phone
- Tom's house

The noun before **'s** is the person or thing that has a relationship with the second noun.

- **John's car** = the car of John

---

## Possessive 's with Singular Nouns

For most singular nouns, add **'s**.

Examples:

- the girl's bag
- the boy's bicycle
- my mother's car
- the teacher's desk
- Daniel's computer

### In a Sentence

The possessive phrase can appear before the noun.

- This is **Anna's book**.
- That is **my brother's room**.
- It is **the teacher's desk**.

---

## Possessive 's with Names

Names normally use **'s** to show possession.

Examples:

- David's phone
- Emma's house
- James's car
- Laura's book

- This is **David's computer**.

---

## Plural Nouns Ending in -s

When a plural noun already ends in **-s**, add only an apostrophe (**'**).

### Structure

- **Plural noun + ' + thing**

Examples:

- the students' books
- the teachers' room
- the girls' bags
- my parents' house

Compare:

- the **girl's** bag → one girl
- the **girls'** bags → two or more girls

---

## Irregular Plural Nouns

Irregular plural nouns do not end in **-s**, so use **'s**.

Examples:

- the children's toys
- the men's clothes
- the women's bags
- the people's opinions

Compare:

- the **boy's** toy → one boy
- the **boys'** toys → several boys
- the **children's** toys → children

---

## Possessive 's vs. Plural -s

The apostrophe is important because **'s** and **s'** can show possession, while **-s** can simply make a noun plural.

| Form   | Meaning                    |
| ------ | -------------------------- |
| girl   | one girl                   |
| girls  | more than one girl         |
| girl's | belonging to one girl      |
| girls' | belonging to several girls |

Examples:

- The **girl's** bag is red.
- The **girls'** bags are red.

---

## Questions About Possession

We can use **whose** to ask who something belongs to.

### Structure

- **Whose + noun + is this/that?**

Examples:

- Whose book is this?
- Whose phone is that?
- Whose car is this?

Answers can use the possessive **'s**:

- It's Maria's.
- It's John's book.
- It's my sister's.

---

## Common Mistakes

### Forgetting the apostrophe

- ❌ Johns car
- ✅ John's car

### Using 's with a plural noun ending in -s

- ❌ the students's books
- ✅ the students' books

### Confusing plural and possession

- ❌ The girls bag is blue.
- ✅ The girl's bag is blue.
- ❌ The girls's bags are blue.
- ✅ The girls' bags are blue.

---

## Quick Summary

- Use **'s** to show possession with singular nouns.
- Use **s'** with plural nouns ending in **-s**.
- Use **'s** with irregular plural nouns.
- **Whose** asks about possession.

### Basic Patterns

- **singular noun + 's + noun**
  - John's car
- **plural noun ending in -s + ' + noun**
  - the students' books
- **irregular plural + 's + noun**
  - the children's toys
