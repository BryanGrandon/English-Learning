## What Does "Have" Mean?

**Have** can be used to talk about possession.

- #### Examples:
  - I have a car.
  - She has a dog.
  - We have a house.
  - They have two children.

The form of **have** changes with **he, she, and it**.

- I/You/We/They **have**
- He/She/It **has**

---

## Have: Affirmative Form

### Structure

- **Subject + have/has + noun**

| Subject | Form | Example                 |
| ------- | ---- | ----------------------- |
| I       | have | I have a car.           |
| You     | have | You have a bike.        |
| He      | has  | He has a dog.           |
| She     | has  | She has a cat.          |
| It      | has  | It has four legs.       |
| We      | have | We have a house.        |
| They    | have | They have two children. |

---

## Have Got

**Have got** can also express possession.

### Structure

- **Subject + have/has got + noun**

Examples:

- I have got a car.
- You have got a bike.
- She has got a cat.
- He has got a new phone.
- We have got a house.
- They have got two children.

The forms are:

- I/You/We/They **have got**
- He/She/It **has got**

---

## Have vs. Have Got

For possession, **have** and **have got** generally express the same basic idea.

Compare:

- I have a car.
- I've got a car.

Both mean that the speaker possesses a car.

More examples:

- She has a dog. = She's got a dog.
- We have a house. = We've got a house.
- They have two children. = They've got two children.

---

## Negative Form with Have

With **have** as a main verb, use **do not / does not**.

### Structure

- **Subject + don't/doesn't + have + noun**

Examples:

- I don't have a car.
- You don't have a bike.
- He doesn't have a dog.
- She doesn't have a computer.
- We don't have a house.

Remember:

- **He doesn't have**
- not: ~~He doesn't has~~

---

## Negative Form with Have Got

With **have got**, put **not** after **have/has**.

### Structure

- **Subject + haven't/hasn't got + noun**

Examples:

- I haven't got a car.
- You haven't got a bike.
- He hasn't got a dog.
- She hasn't got a computer.
- We haven't got a house.

### Full and Contracted Forms

| Full form    | Contracted form |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| have not got | haven't got     |
| has not got  | hasn't got      |

---

## Questions with Have

When **have** is the main verb, use **do/does** to make questions.

### Structure

- **Do/Does + subject + have + noun?**

Examples:

- Do you have a car?
- Do they have children?
- Does he have a dog?
- Does she have a brother?

### Short Answers

- Yes, I do.
- No, I don't.
- Yes, he does.
- No, he doesn't.

Example:

- Do you have a car?
- **Yes, I do.**

- Does she have a dog?
- **No, she doesn't.**

---

## Questions with Have Got

With **have got**, put **have/has** before the subject.

### Structure

- **Have/Has + subject + got + noun?**

Examples:

- Have you got a car?
- Have they got children?
- Has he got a dog?
- Has she got a brother?

### Short Answers

- Yes, I have.
- No, I haven't.
- Yes, he has.
- No, he hasn't.

Example:

- Have you got a car?
- **Yes, I have.**

- Has she got a dog?
- **No, she hasn't.**

---

## Have and Have Got: Comparison

|              | Have                | Have Got             |
| ------------ | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Affirmative  | I have a car.       | I've got a car.      |
| Negative     | I don't have a car. | I haven't got a car. |
| Question     | Do you have a car?  | Have you got a car?  |
| Short answer | Yes, I do.          | Yes, I have.         |

Both structures can express possession.

---

## Common Mistakes

### Using "has" after does

- ❌ Does she has a car?
- ✅ Does she have a car?

### Using "have" with he/she/it

- ❌ She have a dog.
- ✅ She has a dog.

### Mixing the two question structures

- ❌ Do you have got a car?
- ✅ Do you have a car?

or

- ✅ Have you got a car?

### Mixing the negative structures

- ❌ She doesn't has a car.
- ✅ She doesn't have a car.
- ❌ She doesn't have got a car.
- ✅ She hasn't got a car.

---

## Quick Summary

- **Have / has** can express possession.
- **Have got / has got** can also express possession.
- Use **have** with I, you, we, and they.
- Use **has** with he, she, and it.
- With **have**, use **do/does** for questions and negatives.
- With **have got**, use **have/has** directly.

### Basic Patterns

- **I/You/We/They have + noun**
  - I have a car.
- **He/She/It has + noun**
  - She has a car.
- **I/You/We/They have got + noun**
  - I have got a car.
- **He/She/It has got + noun**
  - She has got a car.
