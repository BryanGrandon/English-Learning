Every complete English sentence has a subject and a predicate. Together, they tell us who or what the sentence is about and what the subject does, is, or has.

## What is a Subject?

The subject is the person, animal, place, thing, or idea that the sentence is about.

It often performs the action of the verb.

### Examples

| Sentence                  | Subject    |
| ------------------------- | ---------- |
| I study English.          | I          |
| Maria works at home.      | Maria      |
| The dog runs fast.        | The dog    |
| My friends play football. | My friends |

---

## What is a Predicate?

The predicate tells us what the subject does, is, or has.

The predicate always contains a verb.

### Examples

| Sentence                  | Predicate     |
| ------------------------- | ------------- |
| I study English.          | study English |
| Maria works at home.      | works at home |
| The dog runs fast.        | runs fast     |
| My friends play football. | play football |

- #### Subject + Predicate
  - I + study English.
  - She + is happy.
  - Tom + plays soccer.
  - The children + are in the park.

---

## Finding the Subject

To find the subject, ask:

- Who? or What?

### Example

The boy plays tennis.

- Who plays tennis?
  - The boy is the subject.

The cat sleeps on the sofa.

- Who sleeps on the sofa?
  - The cat is the subject.

---

## Finding the Predicate

To find the predicate, identify the verb and everything that tells us about the subject.

### Example

The boy plays tennis.

- What does the boy do?
  - plays tennis is the predicate.

The cat sleeps on the sofa.

- What does the cat do?
  - sleeps on the sofa is the predicate.

---

## Subject vs. Predicate

| Subject                                       | Predicate                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Who or what the sentence is about             | What the subject does, is, or has |
| Usually contains a noun or pronoun            | Always contains a verb            |
| Comes before the predicate in basic sentences | Usually comes after the subject   |

### Simple Sentences

A simple sentence can have a very short subject and predicate.

- #### Birds fly.
  - Subject: Birds
  - Predicate: fly
- #### John sleeps.
  - Subject: John
  - Predicate: sleeps

The subject and predicate can also be longer.

The small brown dog runs in the park.

- Subject: The small brown dog
- Predicate: runs in the park

### Remember

A complete sentence needs a subject and a predicate.

Subject + Predicate = Complete Sentence

### More Examples

- #### 1. The girl reads a book.
  - Subject: The girl
  - Predicate: reads a book
- #### 2. My brother plays the guitar.
  - Subject: My brother
  - Predicate: plays the guitar
- #### 3. The students are in the classroom.
  - Subject: The students
  - Predicate: are in the classroom
- #### 4. We study English every day.
  - Subject: We
  - Predicate: study English every day
- #### 5. The house is very big.
  - Subject: The house
  - Predicate: is very big

---

## Common Mistake

Do not confuse the subject with the first word of a sentence.

In the morning, John studies English.

The sentence starts with In the morning, but the subject is John.

- Subject: John
- Predicate: studies English
- Extra information: In the morning

### Remember

A complete sentence needs a subject and a predicate.

Subject + Predicate = Complete Sentence

When you look at a basic English sentence, first find who or what the sentence is about. That is the subject. Then find what the subject does, is, or has. That is the predicate.
