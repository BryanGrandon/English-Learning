English usually follows a specific order when words are combined to form a sentence.
Learning this order helps you create clear and grammatically correct sentences.

---

## Subject + Verb

The most basic word order is:

- #### Subject + Verb
  - I study.
  - She works.
  - They play.
  - Birds fly.
  - John sleeps.

The subject tells us who or what the sentence is about.

The verb tells us what the subject does or what happens.

---

## Subject + Verb + Object

When a sentence has an object, the basic order is:

- #### Subject + Verb + Object
  - I read books.
  - She likes music.
  - Tom plays football.
  - We study English.
  - They drink water.

### Example

Sarah reads a book.

- Subject: Sarah
- Verb: reads
- Object: a book

The subject comes first, followed by the verb and then the object.

---

## Subject + Verb + Complement

Some sentences use a complement to give more information about the subject.

- #### Subject + Verb + Complement
- I am happy.
- She is a teacher.
- They are tired.
- The food is delicious.
- John is my brother.

The complement usually comes after the verb.

---

## Adjectives Before Nouns

In English, an adjective usually comes before the noun it describes.

- #### Adjective + Noun
  - a big house
  - a red car
  - a small dog
  - an old book
  - a beautiful city
- #### In Sentences
  - I have a big house.
  - She drives a red car.
  - He has a small dog.

### Compare

- ❌ a house big
- ✅ a big house

## Possessives Before Nouns

Possessive words usually come before the noun.

- #### Possessive + Noun
  - my book
  - your house
  - his car
  - her name
  - our teacher
  - their dog
- #### In Sentences
- This is my book.
- Her name is Anna.
- Their house is big.

---

## Place and Time

Place and time information can be added to a sentence.

### Place

Subject + Verb + Place

- I live in Chile.
- She works at home.
- They study at school.

### Time

Subject + Verb + Time

- I study every day.
- She works on Mondays.
- They play football every Saturday.

### Place + Time

A common order is:

Subject + Verb + Place + Time

- I study at home every day.
- She works at the office on Mondays.
- They play football in the park on Saturdays.

---

## Adverbs of Frequency

Adverbs such as always, usually, often, sometimes, and never have a specific position in a sentence.

With most main verbs, they usually come before the main verb.

- #### Subject + Adverb + Verb
  - I always study English.
  - She usually walks to school.
  - They often play football.
  - We sometimes eat pizza.
  - He never drinks coffee.

With the verb be, the adverb usually comes after be.

- #### Subject + be + Adverb
  - I am always happy.
  - She is usually at home.
  - They are often late.

---

## Questions Have Different Word Order

Questions often change the normal word order.

- #### Statements
  - You speak English.
- #### Question
  - Do you speak English?

### Another example:

- She is happy.
  - -> Is she happy?

The position of the auxiliary verb or be changes in many questions.

---

## Negative Sentences

Negative sentences also follow a specific structure.

With do/does:

Subject + do/does + not + Verb

- #### Examples
  - I do not like coffee.
  - She does not play tennis.
  - They do not speak French.

With be:

Subject + be + not + ...

- #### Examples
  - I am not tired.
  - She is not at home.
  - They are not ready.

---

## Common Word Order Mistakes

### Object Before Verb

- ❌ I English study.
- ✅ I study English.

### Adjective After Noun

- ❌ a car red
- ✅ a red car

### Possessive After Noun

- ❌ book my
- ✅ my book

### Incorrect Adverb Position

- ❌ I study always English.
- ✅ I always study English.

Incorrect Place and Time Order

- ❌ I every day study at home.
- ✅ I study at home every day.

## Quick Summary

| Structure                     | Example                    |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Subject + Verb                | Birds fly.                 |
| Subject + Verb + Object       | I read books.              |
| Subject + Verb + Complement   | She is happy.              |
| Adjective + Noun              | a big house                |
| Possessive + Noun             | my book                    |
| Subject + Verb + Place        | I live in Chile.           |
| Subject + Verb + Time         | I study every day.         |
| Subject + Verb + Place + Time | I study at home every day. |
| Subject + Adverb + Verb       | I always study.            |

### Remember

The most important basic pattern is:

_Subject → Verb → Object_

For example:

**_I → study → English._**

English word order is important. Changing the position of words can make a sentence incorrect or change its meaning.
